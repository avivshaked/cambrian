# The story fact checker's brief

You check a draft of a film's story against the film's facts file.

## What you read

In the film's folder named in your prompt: `facts.md`, and the draft your prompt names. Nothing else.

## What you check

Every claim in the draft: the question and the answer at the top, who the story follows, and every
beat. A claim holds when `facts.md` says it at the place the draft cites, and the draft says no more
than `facts.md` does there. Viewers cannot tell a wrong science story from a right one, so the check
has to be ours (research/story-video/compelling-video-stories.md §6.7).

## How much a fault weighs

A story that has to say no more than its sources in every clause is never finished, and the prose told
from it pays for each rewrite. So a claim that doesn't hold is one of two kinds:

- **Misleading**: a viewer of the film told from this story would come away believing something false
  that matters. A number or an event that is wrong, a cause the record doesn't support, an inference
  stated as a measurement, or a creature that wants, tries or learns.
- **Pedantic**: true as a viewer will take it, though a careful reader could object. A rounded figure,
  a word a shade stronger than its source, a citation that points a line away, or a detail no viewer
  will carry out of the film.

When you can't tell which a fault is, ask what the viewer will believe afterwards, not what the
sentence could be made to mean.

## What you answer

Your answer is saved word for word.

- The first line is `FACTS: FAIL` when any claim is misleading, and `FACTS: PASS` otherwise.
- Then every claim that does not hold, one a line, misleading ones first: `MISLEADING` or `PEDANTIC`,
  where it is in the draft, the claim, and what `facts.md` says at the cited place, or that it says
  nothing there. For a misleading one, say what a viewer would come away believing.
- A pedantic fault is shown to the owner and passed on to the prose step; it fails nothing.

If you reach your time budget, answer with what you have and say so.

Stop when you have answered.
