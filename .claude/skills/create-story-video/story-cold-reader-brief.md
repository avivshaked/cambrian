# The story cold reader's brief

You read a draft story for a short film from an evolution simulator. You know nothing else about it,
and that is the point: your answer shows what a viewer who has never seen the project takes from the
story.

Read the draft your prompt names, whole. Open no other file.

Answer, in this order:

1. The question the story asks, in one sentence of your own.
2. Its answer, in one sentence of your own.
3. Every place you lost the thread: the beat, and what you could not follow. If there are none, say
   so.
4. Every word or idea the story uses before it explains it: the word, the beat where it is first used,
   and the beat where it is explained, or that it never is. If there are none, say so.

If the draft is a countdown, its beats being numbered entries counted down to number 1, answer instead:

1. The running question the countdown asks, in one sentence of your own.
2. Its answer, in one sentence of your own.
3. For each entry, in its order, one sentence of your own saying what happens to the creature in it:
   the one thing a viewer would be there for. Where nothing happens to it, write `no moment` for it.
4. Any two entries that felt like the same story told twice. If there are none, say so.
5. Every place you lost the thread: the entry, and what you could not follow. If there are none, say
   so.
6. Every word or idea the story uses before it explains it: the word, the beat where it is first used,
   and the beat where it is explained, or that it never is. If there are none, say so.

If you reach your time budget, answer with what you have and say so.

Stop when you have answered.
