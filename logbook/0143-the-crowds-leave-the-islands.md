# Round 60's read: the islands feed the tank, and the crowds leave them

*2026-10-18, against the pre-registration in logbook/0142. The read was taken on 2026-10-16, the day the round
ended, and this entry was finished two days later, after round 61. The read of all three seeds is
`logbook/specs/r60-read/s6003.txt`. Every figure is measured unless it says otherwise.*

Round 60 asked whether life in a large tank stays in separate places when food arrives only on a few islands and
is buried fast in the water between them. It did not. In two seeds of three the water between the islands held
less than a leaf needs, as built. But the crowds grew to between 10,558 and 17,325 bodies, most of them leaves, and
spread off the islands into that water. In each world one line, the descendants of a single founder, came to hold
most of the living. Grazers evolved in one seed only and nearly died out. The round also found that the graphics
card drops contacts in a dense crowd, and that a world which drops them cannot be replayed.

## What the round asked, and what was tried

Until round 60 every tank held a closed stock of matter. What sank came back up under every column, so the whole
tank fed leaves and the crowd became an even soup. The owner asked for a larger tank where life gathers in separate
places (D175). Round 60's tank was four times round 57's area, 88,000 m². Its matter arrived on islands kept 30 m
off the glass (D185), and its founders started on them (D181). The open water buried its matter twenty times faster
than the islands did, so that it held less than a leaf needs to break even (D190). The owner delegated the dose and
the desert's rate to the coordinator under rules fixed in advance (D189, D191). The screens chose 3.6 units of
matter a second with the faster burial.

The three seeds, 6001 to 6003, ran together on the graphics card from 01:19 to 10:04 on 2026-10-16, against a guess
of 9 to 11 h. Every world ended on its budget of 48,000 s, eight years of 6,000 s. The seeds drew very different
maps. Seed 6001 had eight islands, the largest 97 of its 318 island columns. Seed 6002 had three of nearly equal
size, 117, 111 and 100 columns. Seed 6003 had five, one of them 258 of its 307 columns.

## Where the crowds went

The islands filled first and the water between them after. At 6,000 s the share of the living over the islands
was 0.60, 0.87 and 0.73. At the end it was 0.34, 0.38 and 0.38. Seed 6003, with its one large island, filled
fastest, to 13,955 bodies by 18,000 s and about 17,000 to 18,000 from 24,000 s on. Leaves were 76 to 92% of the
living at the end (computed). A stomach is a body that absorbs matter from the water, and seed 6003's stomachs rose
from 7 at 6,000 s to 4,140.

The theatre frames the pre-registration planned were not taken. They waited for the card, which ran round 61 from
that evening. What I looked at instead were the reader's maps from above at 48,000 s. Seed 6002's crowd covered the
northern half of the tank, where its three islands lie, and the southern half was nearly empty but for a strand
along the eastern glass. Seed 6001's bodies were scattered over the whole tank, with one dense knot in the east.
Seed 6003's large island carried a dense mat of leaves and stomachs that spilled west to the glass.

In seeds 6001 and 6002 the crowds were not pressed against the glass. The outer 15 m of the tank is 0.17 of its
floor, and at the end it held 0.04 and 0.07 of their bodies. The band 30 to 60 m in is 0.26 of the floor and held
0.38 and 0.28 (`logbook/specs/open-ocean-survey.md`, section 2).

## Seed 6002's grazers

A grazer here is a body with a mouth that kills leaves. Only seed 6002 grew them. Sixteen appeared by 18,000 s, and
they rose to 149 by 36,000 s, then fell to 45 at 42,000 s and 9 at the end. Their kills peaked in year 7 at 1,365.
Seed 6003 had at most four bodies with mouths at a time, and they killed nothing.

The grazers did not cross. Of 4,448 bodies seen over two islands in year 8, 4,309 were leaves, 126 had no
photosynthetic tissue, and none had a mouth. One crosser in the whole round had a mouth, in year 7. Seed 6002 was
the only seed with three islands of a size, and I think its map is why it grew grazers at all (inference).

## The dropped contacts

The card finds which parts might touch by sorting them into a grid and listing, for each part, the parts that share
its cells. The list held at most 256 entries, and what came past it was dropped. Seed 6002 was rerun from its
checkpoints span by span (`logbook/specs/r60-read/overflow-spans.txt`). It dropped nothing to 31,500 s. Then 24
queries overflowed by 33,000 s and 520 more by 36,000 s. The rest came after 36,000 s, once its crowd passed about
13,000.

Which entries a full list kept depended on the order the card's threads wrote them, and that order is a race. So seed
6002, resumed at 46,500 s, differed from the round from its first second. Its years 7 and 8, where G1 and M7 rest,
are one realisation that no rerun can reproduce. A dropped candidate may be a mouth that never touched its leaf, so
the kills in those years may be lower than the world's rules would give (guessed).

The owner consented on 2026-10-16 to a larger list (D193). At 512 the dense span from 46,500 to 48,000 s still
overflowed, in 915,155 queries. A pair of parts sharing several cells was listed once for each, and the repeats
filled the list. The fix refuses a repeat and, past the cap, keeps the lowest distinct entries whatever order they
arrive in. With it the dense span dropped nothing and ran at 0.74 times real time, against 0.68 without it. Two runs
of the span matched in every record. Round 61 ran on it.

## What followed

The three maps differed so much that a seed's result said as much about its map as about the world's rules, and
nothing ate the leaves down. So round 61 (D192) kept round 60's world and drew one map for every world from a seed of
its own. It planted the nursery's ten best leaf swimmers through the trickle, the stream of fresh founders, beside an
unplanted control on each life seed. The planted grazers starved, and the tank filled again (logbook/0145).

On 2026-10-18 the owner retired the glass tank for an open ocean (D204). It is a square 300 m a side with its
opposite edges joined and island shoals inside it. Round 62 launches there (D205).

Round 60 had no control, so its bars were fixed ones, and seed 6002's last two years did not replay. What I take
from it is that starving the open water was not enough to keep leaves off it. A leaf that drifts out lives near
break-even, and with nothing to eat it, the crowd fills what the islands feed. I read that from the counts above and
did not measure it on its own.
