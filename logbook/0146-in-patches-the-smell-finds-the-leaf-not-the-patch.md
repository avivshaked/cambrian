# In patches the smell finds the leaf, not the patch

*2026-10-19, on the nursery's patchy tank, which ran on 2026-10-15 (D182, D184). The plan's step 15
(`logbook/specs/nursery-leaf-tank-plan.md`) holds the full read, and `logbook/specs/nursery-patches-read.txt` the
reads' outputs. Every figure is measured unless it says otherwise.*

The nursery is a small tank where swimmers are bred before they are planted into a world. Until now its food lay
spread through 400 m² of water. Round 60's worlds feed their creatures on islands with bare water between them, so
the owner asked whether a nose pays in a tank laid out the same way. The nursery's tank grew to 3,400 m², and its 31
leaves were gathered into three patches of 6 m radius, with about 24 m of bare water between neighbours. A swimmer
smells a leaf from 6 m. Inside a patch every point smells a leaf, and between patches none does.

## What was bred

The arm, `leaf-direction-patches`, bred bodies and brains together for 20 generations from the ten best swimmers of
the earlier arms. It scored each body on its energy return: what it ate over what it spent. The search did what it
did in the small tank. The bodies grew cheap, spending 137 J an episode where the founders spent 1,063 J, and their
eating held level, between about 65 and 120 J an episode. The best genomes on unseen episodes return 1.24 and 0.98 times what they
spend.

The difference is in the smell. Knocking out the two leaf-smell senses cuts these bodies' eating from 83 J to 9 J,
about nine-tenths of it. In the small tank the same knockout cost about half. With no brain at all they eat 3 J.

## Whether a body keeps to a patch

A forager that has found food should stay near it and come back when it drifts away. The exam's tanks were swum again
under a watcher that records, every step, where each body is and whether it smells a leaf. The ten founders were swum
on the same episodes as a baseline, under their own brains, with no brain, and with the smell knocked out.

| | Time within smell of a leaf | Exits that turn back within 30 s |
|---|---|---|
| Evolved, own brain | 0.44 | 0.48 |
| Evolved, smell knocked out | 0.43 | 0.50 |
| Founders, own brain | 0.41 | 0.48 |
| No brain | 0.40 | 0.51 |

A body with no brain spends four-tenths of its time within smell of a leaf, and the evolved bodies only 0.04 more.
About half of all exits from a patch turn back within 30 s under every brain, the brainless one included. Every body
that started in bare water found a smell, at a median of about three and a half minutes, with or without its nose.
The re-stepped brains matched the exam's record bit for bit, so these are the same swims the exam scored.

My reading, which is inference: in a tank this size, drift alone keeps a body near the patches about as well as any
brain here does. What the smell buys is the last few metres, the turn onto a leaf the body already smells. The
search has not yet bred a forager that holds to a patch, and nothing in this tank asks it to, since drift brings it
back.

## Round 59's planted grazers on the same bench

The same bench answered round 59's clause D2 (logbook/0141): the grazers descended from the planted swimmers, alive
at 48,000 s, swum in the small tank. Their brains doubled their eating, and the smell added little to it. It was
worth 2.1 J of 22.2 J in seed 2 and 1.6 J of 10.1 J in seed 3. Out in the world, then, the planted lines kept their brains but not
much use for the nose.

## What is next

The arm's repeat on a second recipe seed, `leaf-direction-patches-s2-w5`, waits for the coordinator; the grazer screen
holds the efficiency cores first. If the repeat agrees, the open question is what would make holding to a patch
pay. Two candidates are patches farther apart than drift carries a body, and food that runs out where many feed.

A personal note: I expected the patches to breed homebodies. The table above is the first time a brainless control
in the nursery told me that a behaviour I was about to credit to evolution was the tank's.
