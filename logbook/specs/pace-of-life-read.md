# Pace of life: one scheme (a design read, 2026-10-25)

A Fable subagent's report, asked for by the owner on 2026-10-25 (a universal fix so every timed action runs at the pace of metabolism). Saved as it came by the coordinator; cut short at the coordinator's request to save tokens. Read-only: nothing run, no JSONL opened. KNOW = code or committed record; BELIEVE = reasoned from those; GUESS = a number with no measurement. The grazer budget (`logbook/specs/grazer-breeding-budget.md`) was read in full and its figures are used. The GPU water seam and the farm's growth cadence are read from grep hits, not whole files, and are marked.

## 1. The recommendation in plain words

Give the world two clocks and one number between them. The physics keeps its clock exactly as it is (0.01 s steps, the same forces, the same bits). The economy keeps its clock exactly as it is (0.5 s metabolic steps, every rate in Core unchanged). The one new number, the **pace factor k**, says how many physics seconds pass in one world second. At k = 1 nothing changes and every recorded world replays. At k = 3, a metabolic step of 0.5 world-seconds holds 150 physics steps instead of 50, so in the time a body burns one meal it swims, sinks, strokes and touches three times as much. Nothing in the economy moves: upkeep, the mouth, healing, growth, gestation, the age hazard and the remains window are all written against the world's seconds (every one takes a `seconds` argument from `World.Step`), so they stay in pace with each other by construction. The only things that change pace are the physical actions, which is exactly the class the owner named as too slow.

Why this and not a thrust or drag multiplier: a multiplier on the push makes a stick swim like a paddle and so erases the selection for fins that D229 and D231 were just built to create; a lower drag coefficient changes how leaves drift and sink, which is what makes the oases (D209). The pace factor changes no force, so every relative physical incentive stays as it is: a paddle still beats a stick five to seven times (the fin bench), and under k = 3 the stick grazes and the paddle can migrate. I BELIEVE that is the ocean the owner asked for. Its honest cost is wall time, times k, because the physics is where the compute is.

One companion dial is needed whatever the scheme: `NewbornReserveFraction` from 0.2 to about 0.5, because a newborn's 13.5 J lasts 15 s (0.3 clocks) and no speed makes one search shorter than that (the budget's section 3).

A ballistic check that the encounter model is right (BELIEVE): about 10,000 leaves over the islands' ~35,000 m³ give n ≈ 0.3 m⁻³; with a mouth cross-section σ ≈ 0.5 m² and v = 0.05 m/s, nσv ≈ 0.0075 s⁻¹, a search of ~130 s, which matches the measured 95-107 s. So search is set by n, σ, v and nothing else, and the cap, the waste and the kill rule cannot move it, as the kill-rule read said.

How k follows: Λ scales as k; Λ_target/Λ_today = 2.5, so k ≈ 2.5 before the work term. Under the pace factor a swimmer's billed work per clock rises as k (it strokes k times as long per clock at the same stroke; `Creature.Ledger.cs:177` sums |τ·ω|·dt per physics step), not as k³, because no extra thrust is bought. With W = 0.09k W: at k = 3, c_min = 0.908 + 0.27 + 2.5·175.5/3000 = 1.32 W, t_max = 35 s, search 33 s: passes. At k = 2.5 it fails by a hair (t_max 36 s, search 40 s). At k = 4, c_min 1.41 W, t_max 33 s, search 25 s: comfortable. Recommend k = 3, screen 2 and 4 beside it. The budget's "price must fall as 1/k²" is what the pace factor does for free: its k³ thrust law times 1/k² is this scheme's k.

Newborn under k = 3: needs U·(s/k + 1 s) ≈ 0.91 × 34 ≈ 31 J; at fraction f the child's reserve lasts about 59·f/(1−f) s (BELIEVE, from the budget's 67.5 J share and upkeep by volume): f = 0.5 gives 59 s, 1.3 clocks, one search with margin.

## 3. The schemes compared

| Scheme | One factor | Speed per clock | Billed work per clock | Physically real? | Books | Wall time | Evolution |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a1) thrust ×k² (`LimbPushCoefficient`, or Ω×k) | yes, plus a price change | ×k | ×k² (C_T) or ×k³ (Ω); needs `WorkCost`/k² | push k² above the drag law: thinner-than-water fins | close | ×1 | erases the fin incentive; strokers only |
| (a2) drag Cd/k² | yes | ×k swimmers | ×1 | Cd 0.17 is realistic for a fish, wrong for a broadside leaf | close | ×1 | leaves sink ×k, lag the eddies 10×; re-asks D209's oases |
| (a3) mouth, growth, healing rates | many | none | – | – | – | – | eating is 0.02 clocks already: no lever |
| (b) slow metabolism (every Core rate /k) | one factor, many sites | ×k | ×k | real: a colder animal | close | ×k | all incentives kept |
| (c) shrink spacing (denser leaves, smaller world) | ecology, not a dial | ×1, but d/L falls | ×1 | real | close | ×1 | denser water is less ocean-like; migration unhelped |
| (d) pace factor: world second = k physics seconds | **one tunable, one seam** | ×k everything physical | ×k | real: a time-lapse | close, untouched | ×k (×1.5 with dt 0.02) | all incentives kept |
| (e) directed search: nose (D235), fat (D229), fins | ruled already | σ ×10-100 | – | the most real | close | ×1 | the biological answer; slow to arrive |

(a1) is the budget's "price 1/k²" made explicit. It is cheap and wall-free, but a universal push multiplier is the same gift to every limb, so the stick-vs-paddle difference the fin law created is divided out of what matters, and a thrust k² above drag is a fiction a physicist will not accept. (a2) is the one knob that makes swimmers more real (bluff Cd 1.5 is the unreal number for a fish), but Cd is also what ties leaves to the water, and the oases live on that tie. (b) and (d) are the same physics in different words; (d) is (b) with every Core rate left alone and the conversion made once, where the two clocks meet. (c) is the real ecology's answer inside an oasis and costs nothing, but it is not a dial and does not help travel between oases. (e) is already ruled and should go on; the pace factor makes it pay sooner.

What becomes unreal under (d): nothing in the forces. Per world second a body sinks k times as far (excess density 0.02, `Fluid.cs:339-360`), a brain thinks k times as often for the same neural bill, and the drive's hold/reaction/release genes and `PoseHertz`/Ω, which tick in physics seconds (`EffectorDrive.cs:295-311`), run k times faster per clock. Those are the time-lapse's own consequences and I would leave them.

## 5. The owner's decisions

1. **Which scheme.** Recommended: the pace factor (d). Options: (a1) thrust ×k² with the price /k² — wall-free, but a physics fiction that cancels the fin incentive; (a2) Cd/k² — more real for swimmers, re-asks the oases; (b) is (d) in many sites; (c) denser leaves — no dial, no migration. Implications of (d): a new tunable in the `physics` group, so every older `config.json`, the r42 thread-identity config, `RunFixture`'s crowd and the checkpoint fixtures are refused and re-recorded (the standard cost); Core untouched except reading the factor; the farm's `ResolvePhysicsStep` and `Simulation` take `50k` steps per metabolic step; the solver's clock and water sample converted at one seam on the host and the same seam in the kernel; a card identity sitting at two group sizes. Build: a Sonnet afternoon plus the fixture recordings; GUESS.
2. **The value of k.** Recommended 3, with 2 and 4 as the screen's arms. k = 2.5 just fails the adult target by the budget's arithmetic; 4 leaves room for the typical grazer whose search may be several times the world mean (the budget's caveat).
3. **The newborn reserve.** Recommended `NewbornReserveFraction` 0.5 with k = 3 (0.2 today). It moves every lineage's children, leaves included, so it is a world rule. Without it bred grazers still die at 15 s whatever k does.
4. **Paying for the wall.** Recommended: accept ×k for the screen (two worlds a night at the card's pace, which is how `r62kg` is already shaped), and run one arm at dt 0.02 to test recovering half of it. Implication: dt above 0.01 engages the drag and drive limiters (`SolverConfig.cs:379-385`) and is a new realisation; the D199 blade limiter was built for it but it has not run in a round.
5. **The current in world seconds.** Recommended: yes, converted at the seam, so leaves and matter drift exactly as today per clock. The alternative, the current at its physical 0.02 m/s per physics second, is 0.06 m per world second at k = 3, which D209 found breaks the oases (0.1 m/s carried island leaves off five to ten times faster).

Rules chosen for cheapness that k re-asks: D209's current (above); D064's neutral volume and 2% excess density (sinking per clock ×k; read `meanHeight` and the bed share); the growth cadence of 10 s (now 30 physics seconds between resizes); `PositionsEvery` 100 s sampling (bodies move k× between samples, so `travel.py` undercounts path more); the pace instruments (`x real time` must be read as world seconds per wall second; `pace-check.py` compares a run only with itself, so it holds); the theatre's live cousin and the nursery tanks need the factor; the drive envelope genes in physics seconds (left as they are, said above).

## 6. The screen

One card group, `r62cs-w500`'s world with the gut as `r62kg` ruled it, seeds 6201 and 6202. Arms: k = 2, 3 and 4 at `NewbornReserveFraction` 0.5; k = 3 at 0.2 (so the two dials are separable); k = 3 at dt 0.02. Controls, not run again: `r62cs-w500`, `r62bd-x2`, `r62kg`. Each world to 24,000 world seconds, two worlds a night. Before the group: the identity round with k = 1 bit-identical to the gut build, and k = 3 identical at groups 32 and 64.

Deciding numbers, read at 24,000 s by `r62-read.ps1 -Worlds`: D222's tests 1 and 2; **grazer contact share** (biting steps per grazer-second, from `mouthBlows`) and **kills a grazer-second**, which must rise about k-fold over `r62cs-w500` if the encounter model is right; intake over upkeep; bred grazers' median age (must leave 15-20 s); and the migrants per year from `r60-read.py`, with the oases' island share (M3's 0.6) as the overshoot guard.

I would be shown wrong if: kills a grazer-second rise much less than k (then search is not ballistic in v, and the nose, not the pace, is the lever); if intake over upkeep rises with k but the newborn arm at 0.2 breeds as well as 0.5 (then the newborn term was not binding); if the island share falls under 0.6 at k = 3 with the current converted (then drift per clock was not the only tie); or if the k = 4 arm passes tests 1 and 2 while k = 3 fails both (then the typical grazer's search is well above the mean and k belongs nearer 5). Watch a k = 3 world in the theatre before the write-up: bodies should visibly cover ground between leaf columns, and a paddle body, if one appears, should cross between islands.

Not verified for want of reading: the GPU water seam's exact lines, `_sinceGrowthStep`'s clock, and whether `World.ElapsedSeconds` is what the farm hands the solver's pin at a resume.
