# Leaf scent literature: candidate pool

The searches ran on 2026-10-12, and the search log is `search-log.md` beside this file. Every entry below came from a search, a snowball list or a database result in this session, and each was verified on 2026-10-12 (see the log). This file is the pool. What was read in full, and what each source says on each question, is in `pass2.md`. **No finding here goes beyond what the line's source states.**

**How to read the columns.**

- **Source of the line**: *abstract* means the abstract was read in this session through CrossRef, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex; the text is kept in `verify.json` and `abstracts.txt`. *Full text* means the paper was also read in pass 2, and `pass2.md` gives the locators. *Search summary* means only the web search tool's summary was seen, which is weaker.
- **Verified in**: CR is CrossRef, S2 is Semantic Scholar, EPMC is Europe PMC and OA is OpenAlex. Each entry matched on title, first author and year in every database listed. None carries a CrossRef correction or retraction notice, and none is flagged retracted in OpenAlex.
- **Open access**: what Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex flag. *Yes* means at least one flags it, *no* means neither does. The flag was not always tested by fetching.
- **Found by**: the search tag in `search-log.md` (Q, E, W), or *snowball* with its seed.
- **Age**: entries before 2001 are marked *foundational*: each is the origin of a method or result later work uses, or the only source found for its point.
- The "Why a candidate" text after the source line is my inference about how the paper bears on a leaf scent. It is not a claim the paper makes.

The pool has 43 entries, a little above the 40 the brief named. Thirteen more were verified and not carried; they are listed at the end with the reason.

## Strongest for the questions

*This ranking is my reading, not a finding.*

- **Q1:** C1 and C2 are the two direct tests of a herbivore finding a plant, and they disagree in an informative way (still water against quiet or flowing water). C11 is the strongest for plankton. C5, C6 and C7 show discrimination by odour alone in snails and an amphipod.
- **Q2:** C16, C17 and C18 for damage-released cues; C2, C20 and C21 for continuous release from intact tissue and how it scales.
- **Q3:** C28, C29 and C30 are full texts with numbers; C27 is the frame.
- **Q4:** C34 and C2.
- **Q5:** C35 and C12 for thresholds, C37 and C38 for decay.
- **Q6:** C28 and C40 for body span; C30 and C41 for tropotaxis and rheotaxis in practice; C43 and C36 for separate channels.

## Where the evidence looked thin

- **Herbivorous fish.** Nothing found on how they find algae, by smell or by sight (Q1e, W16).
- **Vision in grazers.** No source on grazers locating algae by sight was found in any search. The question was asked, and the absence is a gap in this search, not evidence that vision is unused.
- **Sea urchins at a distance.** Only stimulation assays at close range (C9, C10) and a DMSP feeding test (C16). A review cited in a search summary says urchins orient upstream to distant food, which was not verified.
- **Cue concentrations from intact plants.** Bulk dissolved organic carbon is measured (C20, C21), but the share of it that animals sense is not.
- **Seagrass and freshwater plants.** Only diatoms on seagrass (C19) and freshwater biofilm algae (C5, C6, C18).
