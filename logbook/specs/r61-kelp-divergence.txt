r61-p6103x2's divergences, diagnosed off the card on 2026-10-18 (evolution-simulator-08)
Run: runs/r61-p6103x2/2026-10-18-043434-119d0f30, read live to t 55,120 s (121 diverged by then; 53 dump files,
50 single frames and 3 traces). Nothing on the card and nothing in the run was touched.

In short: every blow-up is a kelp-family blade. Its spin squares itself in a few steps because the water's drag is
stepped explicitly and the drag limiter is off at this step. The rate does not track the giant count.

1. Who diverges
- All 50 single-frame dumps are the kelp family, and so are the 3 traces. Each root is a photosynthetic box
  with Indeterminate growth and a recursive edge whose scale grows the length 1.6 to 2.3 times a module (1.96 in most)
  and narrows the width (x0.6-1.0) and the thickness (x0.3-0.6).
- The thickness stops at a 0.02 m floor, so each module is a longer, narrower plate 0.02 m thick, and mass grows
  about 1.45x a module.
- Rigid chains, all of them: 47 have 0 dof (Fixed joints alone). The 3 traced bodies carry one spherical link (3 dof)
  whose joint velocity is 0 in every frame.
- At death 46 of the 50 hold 7 or 8 parts.
- Grown from the last snapshot before death, which is often a module short, they are straight blades whose longest
  part is 10-50 m. 108599 has 8 parts and its last is 41.6 x 0.057 x 0.02 m. Moments about the transverse axes run
  1e2 to 1.8e4 kg m2, about the long axis 1 to 30.
  (scratch/r61-giants/dumps-p6103x2.txt, GiantFind --dumps)
- One dump is not a blow-up: 120254, a one-part body of the family found inside reef 46 by 0.19 m.
- 52 are blow-ups: 51 by "link 0's contact sphere out of the grid's reach" and one, 119669, by its root height
  (1.1e15 m).

2. What blows up first: the spin, squaring each step
- Trace 108737 holds the last three steps before its dump. Link 0's angular velocity is 3.7e3, then 4.4e7, then
  3.8e15 rad/s, each about 3 times the square of the one before. Velocity and force follow.
- That is the signature of an explicit step on a drag that grows as the square of the spin. Once kappa*dt*|w|
  passes 2, each step overshoots and the error squares.
- A contact push grows with the depth it pushes out of, not with the spin squared. A joint cannot do it (0 dof, or
  joint velocity 0). Nothing in the giants' contacts enters it.
- The single frames agree: velocity is position/dt in every dump, so the body left a sane place in one step.

3. The harness: the same bodies alone on the CPU solver (GiantFind --spin)
Setup: each body grown from the last snapshot before its dump, alone in open water at the run's fluid settings,
contacts off, with a starting spin about one world axis on its root, run for up to 3 s. Recorded: the smallest
starting spin (ladder 0.02-20 rad/s) at which it runs past 1e6 rad/s.

- The threshold falls with the blade's length, from 20 rad/s at 1.4 m to 0.5 rad/s at 50 m. A 50 m blade turning
  half a radian a second, its tip moving 12 m/s, blows up in eight steps.
- Each drag term alone (the panels' pressure drag, D161's spin drag) is enough at about twice the spin.
- Halving the step about doubles the threshold, as an explicit step's would.
- With no drag the solver holds every body to 20 rad/s for well over a hundred steps. The rigid integration is not
  the cause.
- Caveat: the snapshot bodies are often a module short of the bodies at death, and a module about doubles the
  length, so the thresholds at death are lower still (inference).

4. Why nothing catches it
- The water does have a limiter for this (src/Evosim.Dynamics/Fluid.cs). It caps a link's opposing drag at m*v/dt,
  and its opposing turning torque at its smallest inertia times its spin over dt.
- But SolverConfig.DragLimiterEngages is StepSeconds > 0.0100001, and the world steps at 0.01 s, so the limiter never
  engages. Every report reads "drag limited 0" for that reason.
- The card's water and fluid pass computes this drag in this world. The harness ran the CPU solver's Fluid.cs. That
  the card does the same arithmetic is inference from the gates that hold the two bit-identical.

5. Does the rate track the giants? No
- Pearson r is 0.11 between the divergence rate and the count of bodies with a row of 1,024 cells or more, over 22
  windows from 49,600 to 55,000 s (scratch/r61-giants/rate-vs-giants.txt).
- The rate rose from about 10 per 1,000 s (49,600-51,000) to 20-27 (51,000-55,000). The giant bodies rose to about
  140 at 50,800 s, fell to 80 by 53,000 and climbed to 180 by 55,000.
- A giant is a size, and the blow-up is a spin that falls with length. A count of 7- and 8-module blades would be the
  covariate to try (not measured).
- What gives a blade its first half radian a second is not identified: a contact, a reef or the bed, the current, or
  a module added. The three traces show no resize in their frames.

6. What a fix would be (each one changes the realisation, so each is the owner's)
- (a) Engage the drag limiter at 0.01 s as well. It is per link, and a 0-dof blade turns as one rigid body, so whether
  a per-link cap holds a 40 m chain needs the harness (not tested).
- (b) Step the drag implicitly, or split the step for a body whose drag-to-inertia ratio passes a bound.
- (c) A world rule on the blade itself: a limit on a module's growth in length, or on the thin floor's aspect.
- (d) Leave it. The blow-up then works as a death that falls on the longest kelp blades, an unphysical size cap on
  the line.
