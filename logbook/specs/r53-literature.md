## 4. Synthesis by question, and what it implies for the proposal

**C1. Decay while sinking against after settling.** Measured: microbial rates do not rise on settling. Sinking aggregates respire 0.08-0.20/d [IP10 p.1]; settled phytodetritus turns over at 0.04/d (0.05-0.1/d in other fjords) and settled jellyfish at 0.016/d without scavengers [SW16 p.9]. A small carcass in warm water loses half its dry weight in about 8 h while still in the water [EL10, abstract]. Keeping pellets falling sped their release a little and did nothing for carcasses [LF94, abstract]. Animals are what is fast on the bed: scavengers arrive in about 1 h [JO98] and take a whole jellyfish in 2.5 h [SW14 p.1, p.6], and the bed's respiration responds within 2-24 h [SW16 p.1; MO05 p.1]. Inferred (arithmetic across systems): scavenger removal is several hundred times faster than microbial turnover of the same material (2.5 h against a 43 d half-life). For Rule A: "faster decay once settled" has no support as a microbial process; it has support as a stand-in for the bed community's rapid processing. The dissolved route is a modelling shortcut, and the natural analogue of a fast settled rate is consumption, not leaching (inference). The premise that a corpse carries its joules down holds for large, fast-sinking items (jelly at 1500 m/d [SW14 p.1]) and not for small ones, which lose much of their mass in the water column [EL10]. The sediment's processing has a ceiling of 2.5-4.9 g C m-2 d-1 [SW16 p.9, cited], so a per-corpse rate that ignores pile size will be optimistic for big piles (inference). The proposal's 13x ratio between settled and sinking rates has no measured counterpart.

**C2. Hotspots.** Measured: enrichment is local (soil changes about 2 m from an elephant carcass [BA13 p.9, secondary]; seep carbon in the fauna to about 10 m [LE16 p.6]) and long-lived on land (2-3 years [BE20 p.3]); its spatial extent is "not well documented" [BE20 p.2]. Only 1 of 4 large fish carcasses produced seabed enrichment [HG14 p.5]. In the sea, carcass movement diffuses activity in 3-D [BEA12, abstract]. Carcasses are about 4% of the POC flux locally [HG14 p.1]. No measurement of the dissolved plume round a carcass in the water column was found. Inferred: the simulator's finding that a cell's water holds about 0.26% of the pile has no direct test, but agrees in direction with nature, where most of a carcass reaches animals and not the water.

**C3. Scavengers against microbes.** Measured: microbes compete by making carrion repugnant; fresh carrion drew 2.6x as many animals, and spoiled carrion was 4x more likely to go uncolonised [BU06, abstract; JA77; BE19 p.12]. Warmth and season shift the division [DV03; TU17, abstracts]. Dispersion decides which consumers win [WI03 p.2]. For Rule A: the trade between mouths and stomachs has a measured natural analogue; fast decay takes the meal from mouths [BU06]. Screen S4 and round 52's mouth reading are the right tests (inference).

**C4. Still feeders.** Measured: feeders deplete their own boundary layer, and income rises with flow speed and turbulence, which refill it [FR89, abstract]. Refiltration reaches 18%, set by the feeding current against the ambient shear [OR93, abstract]. Deposit feeders are buffered by buried stock [LE72]. The simulator's emptied cell (a third to a half of its neighbours) is the concentration boundary layer, realistic and not a bug (inference). The natural levers are faster or rougher flow, a feeding current, height above the bed, or moving; which to give the world is the owner's.

**C5. Vents and seeps.** Measured: a seep's influence reaches about 10 m in the sediment fauna and up to 2 km through mobile predators [LE16 p.6]. Where photosynthetic food is available (a 30-50 m shelf), almost no fauna use seep carbon [LE16 p.6]. Vent fields are metacommunities, with larval retention at about 100 m, decadal disturbance and fast pioneer recolonisation [MU18 p.4; VR10]. For a charging seep: a single point source is a real analogue; it builds a distinct community mainly where light-derived food is scarce, so it pays on a dark bed and much less under a productive crowd (inference). More than one seep, with dispersal between them, is what the literature treats as a system (inference).

**C6. Structure.** Measured: depressions and canyons trap detritus and fuel patches of very high secondary production [VE95; VD99; DL10, abstracts; 100-fold biomass in DL10]. Pits are predicted sites of deposition [JU90]. Topographic heterogeneity is strongly and positively related to richness on land [ST14]. Hollows are well supported as traps; the diversity claim rests on terrestrial meta-analyses plus marine case studies, and [VD99] shows currents in structured topography also disturb.

**C7. Depth and coupling.** Measured: shelves under 50 m (2% of the ocean) take about 48% of the flux to the seafloor [DU07, abstract]; benthic biomass tracks flux [WE10 p.1]; processing of fresh detritus is the same shallow and deep at equal temperature [MO05 p.1]; jelly scavenging showed no depth effect from 250 to 1250 m [DU17]. Rule B is directionally supported. The tank is shelf-scale at any depth tried, and its losses on the way down come from its own rule, not from ocean-scale attenuation (inference). The attenuation laws (Suess, Martin) were not read and must not be quoted.

**C8. Models.** Measured, from abstracts: explicit detritus stabilises food-web models [MB04]; green and brown chains couple through nutrient cycling [ZO16]; resource pulses are rare, intense and brief [YA08]; carcass dispersion changes who dominates [WI03]; obligate marine scavenging is feasible [RH04a]. No artificial-life system was found that studied decomposition, scavenging or bed hotspots as a variable (C8e, 69 records); Oohashi's SIVA is a lead.

**Anchoring.** No searched paper addressed it directly. [OR93] and [FR89] imply that a fixed feeder depends on the flow past it (inference).

## 5. Gaps and threats to validity

- Semantic Scholar was unavailable (429 on every call): no influential-citation counts, and discovery rests on OpenAlex alone. The two-database rule was met for verification (OpenAlex plus CrossRef), not for discovery.
- The load-bearing papers are mostly read at abstract level: 12 of 31 appraised were read in PDF. Every claim marked "abstract" must be read in full before it sets a number.
- The one direct sinking-against-still comparison [LF94] is abstract-only and 30 years old; nothing measured carcass decay before and after settling on the same material.
- Mismatch of scale: the literature is deep-sea, days to months; the tank is 25-45 m with time compressed. Only ratios and directions transfer, and even those cross systems.
- The microbe-scavenger evidence is one coastal experiment [BU06] plus terrestrial reviews; Sherratt 2006 may contest Janzen.
- Heterogeneity and diversity is terrestrial [ST14]; the marine support is canyon studies from one school (Vetter, Smith, De Leo).
- Concentration: Sweetman is on three of the C1/C3 papers and Smith's group on four.
- No measured extent of a dissolved carcass plume, no ALife precedent, and no study of anchoring.
- Retrieval integrity: one repository record served the wrong paper and was caught only by the first-page check.
