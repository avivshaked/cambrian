# Round 48: the eaters faded, and a leaf kept a stomach

*The story behind round 48's second film, written for a reader who has not seen the project.
Every number is read from the three runs by `make_story.py` and `facts.py` beside this file, and
every number on screen has a row in `checks.tsv`. Where I am guessing, the text says so. The words
are the glossary's (`glossary.md`).*

Round 48 changed four rules to give eaters a chance. An eater is a body with a stomach and no leaf,
living on the snow of dead matter that drifts through the water. The eaters came, and at first they did well. Stored eaters
dropped into the tanks now lived about five minutes where they used to die within one. They bred,
and one line of them reached 45 bodies. Then every eater line died out, the last at 10,912 s. What
lasted was quieter. In the first tank a leaf was born with a speck of stomach. At the end its line was the
largest line carrying a stomach in any tank, 29 bodies living almost wholly on light.

The arc is bittersweet. We hoped for eaters and got a leaf that keeps a stomach it barely uses,
which is the smaller of the round's two hopes and the only one that came true.

## A tank lives on light, and a body lives on its reserve

Each tank is a disc of water 167 m across, lit from above. Sunlight fades fast with depth: at
12 m about 14% of the surface light is left. Flat rock tables near the surface shade the water
under them.

A body is built from a recipe that its children inherit with small changes, and recipes that
leave more children become common. The film teaches the rest on one body, number 201 in the first
tank. It has two parts, a leaf and a stomach 0.24% of its size. At 2,500 s it earned 0.54 J a
second, nearly all of it from light, and spent 0.49 J: 0.41 J of upkeep and 0.08 J leaked from its
leaf into the water. What was left went into its reserve, 45 J at that moment.

Its whole life is in the stomach log, one row every ten seconds, and it answers two of the owner's
questions. Body 201 was born at 523 s at 40% of its adult size. For its first 27 s it put its
savings into growing, because a body below adult size spends everything above a tenth of its
body's worth on growth. Once full grown it saved, and at 850 s its reserve covered the price of two
children plus the cushion its recipe insists on keeping, 51 s of upkeep, and it had both at once.
The pair cost 129 J and left it holding its 14 J cushion.

Each child's price has three parts. The parent gives each child a share of its own body's worth
(its recipe's investment, 0.72, times its 41 J body, split between two). Four fifths of that share
build the child's body, 11.7 J, and one fifth is the child's first reserve, 2.9 J. On top comes a
fee of twice the child's body, never less than 50 J, which is burnt. So one child cost 64.7 J, and
50 J of it was the fee.

Upkeep rises with age, doubling by 3,000 s. By 3,000 s body 201 had also drifted from 3 m down to
14 m, where its leaf caught half the light it had, and its income fell below its upkeep. It starved
at 3,206.5 s, when its reserve reached zero. That is the only way anything died in round 48: no
living body was eaten, and none broke.

## The round hoped for a line of eaters

Snow is what a stomach eats. Three quarters of it is leaked by living leaves, 15% of the light
they catch, and a quarter comes from the dead as their corpses crumble. The first film said the
dead make the snow; the counters say the leaves make three times as much (75.4%, 73.8% and 75.9%
in the three tanks).

A line, in these films, is a family: a first body and every descendant born with the same kinds of
part. A child that gains or loses a leaf, a stomach or a joint starts a new line. Round 47 had no
line of eaters that lasted, and the stored eaters it dropped in died within a minute (a median of
46, 24 and 42 s). So round 48 ran with four new rules (D119 to D122).

1. A new kind of part arrives only as a small bud on a working body.
2. A child's fee grows with its size.
3. Age raises upkeep but no longer cuts income.
4. A newcomer lands at the depth where its food is richest, with a gift of energy.

 The round's two hopes, among its fifteen predictions, were a line of eaters with ten
living members at the end (F3) and a leaf that grew a stomach and founded such a line (M3).

## Stored eaters arrived well and bred

After 3,000 s a random newcomer drops into each tank about every 30 s, and one in ten is a copy of
an eater we stored from earlier rounds. Body 3318 landed in the third tank at 3,343.5 s, a single
round stomach with no leaf. It came with 66 J of its own and a gift of 72 J, 600 s of its upkeep at
the size it landed at. Half a second later it was full grown (30 J of growth) and had a child
(69 J). That was 99 J, more than its own 66 J, so the gift paid for its first child. The first film
guessed this and had not checked it. At 3,500 s it ate 1.25 J a second and paid 0.50 J, and in the
run it had another child 11.5 s later.

Its line, Gastrella cidutis, had 24 bodies at 4,000 s and peaked at 26 at 4,049 s. The names are
the guide's. The first word says what kind of body a line is: a Gastrella is an eater, a Frondium
or a Thallus a leaf, a Vorafrons a leaf with a stomach. The second word is drawn from the first
body's recipe. Across the three tanks a typical stored eater now lived about five minutes (a median
of 312, 298 and 335 s), and 56 of them bred.

The first tank drew the same stored recipe at 4,057 s as body 4185. It too bred half a second after
landing. Two earlier lines in that tank already carried the name, so this one is Gastrella cidutis
III. It reached 45 bodies at 5,903.5 s, the largest eater line of any tank. The same tank had a
jointed eater line, Natogastrum plocrele, whose bodies are a stomach and a jointed limb. In round 48
a limb part also catches light at half a leaf's rate. So these jointed eaters earned part of their
living from light: at 5,000 s the line's first body took 0.35 J a second of light beside 0.47 J of
snow.

## Every eater line died out

At 6,500 s body 3559 was the last living member of Gastrella cidutis. It was 2,655 s old and paid
0.73 J a second, where its line's first body, young, had paid 0.50 J. The snow where it floated was
thinner, 0.61 J/m³ against 1.39 when 3318 landed, and it ate 0.55 J a second. Its reserve was 9 J.
In the run it starved 44.5 s later, and the line ended.

Gastrella cidutis III was gone by 8,214 s and Natogastrum plocrele by 10,912 s. At the end no
stored eater had a living descendant. The only living bodies from the store were stored eaters
themselves, newly dropped in.

I don't know why the eater lines faded. My guess, from those two bodies and not from a
measurement across the lines, is that each crowd thinned the snow around itself while its members
aged into higher upkeep.

## Meanwhile two leaf lines bet on opposite children

The first tank's middle hours belonged to two leaf lines founded 1.5 s apart. Frondium marufubens
made two big children at a time (a median investment of 0.91 over a brood of two), born at a median
38% of their adult size. Frondium nisimocrax made one small child (0.07), born at 6%. At 10,000 s
they held 1,315 and 1,386 of the tank's 2,859 bodies. Marufubens peaked at 1,441 at 11,460 s and
fell to 2 by the end. Nisimocrax ended with 8,461 of 8,618.

The third tank ran the same contest and the big children won. Thallus legatribens, born at 59% of
adult size, ended with 6,165; Thallus migugis, born at 9%, ended with 2,340. In both tanks the
winner already led at 2,500 s, by 773 to 134 and by 280 to 30. I don't know why the same pair of
bets won in opposite tanks, and my guess is that an early lead mattered more than the bet.

This subplot feeds the main one. The leaf that grew the lasting stomach was a nisimocrax leaf.

## A leaf grew a speck of stomach and kept it

At 23,300.5 s nisimocrax leaf 44820 had a child, body 48048, born with a bud: a small new part of
another kind, here a stomach. Round 48's first rule made a bud the only way a new kind of part can
arrive. At 27,500 s body 48048 was 4,200 s old, more than twice the tank's average age of 1,894 s.
It lived at the surface and had had six children. Its stomach brought in 0.13% of its income, and
light the rest. It is body 201's kit again, a leaf with a speck of stomach, 22,800 s later.

Its line, Vorafrons vabecrepis, ended with 29 living members, and nine more descendants had shed
the stomach and so started lines of their own. No tank ended with a larger line carrying a stomach.
Three other stomach buds on nisimocrax leaves also left ten or more living descendants (31, 10 and
10), none of them descended from another. So the second hope, M3, held in the first tank. Its best
budded line in the second tank reached 9 and in the third 6.

The stomach is 0.17% of a median member's body and brings in 0.24% of its income. I read the line
as a leaf carrying a stomach that costs it almost nothing. That would explain why selection has
not removed it, and it is a guess.

## What the first film got wrong

The code and the counters contradict the first film in six places, and the second film says the
corrected thing.

1. It called a link "a plain part that a joint can swing". In round 48 a link also catches light at
   half a leaf's rate (`photosyntheticEfficiency` 0.025 against 0.05).
2. It said dead bodies drift down as snow. The dead make a quarter of the snow; living leaves leak
   the other three quarters.
3. It said the tank is 45 m deep. That is the mean; the floor slopes from 1 m to about 93 m.
4. It said nisimocrax parents "put about 8% of an adult into a child" and marufubens "91% of an
   adult into the pair". The investment is a share of the parent's own body's worth, and a fifth
   of each child's share becomes its reserve; the child's own size at birth is a separate reading
   (6% and 38%). The tank's "mean investment" is that recipe number averaged over the living.
5. It said nothing new arrives except the newcomers' starting gifts. A newcomer's whole body and
   its 200 J-times-size start arrive from outside too, and light brings energy all the time; only
   the matter is closed, apart from what newcomers carry in.
6. It guessed that the gift paid for body 3318's first child and had not checked. It did: growing
   up and the child cost 99 J against 66 J of its own.

Three smaller numbers moved when I read the lineage birth by birth instead of from the guide's
ten-second positions. Gastrella cidutis III peaked at 5,903.5 s (the first film said 5,910 s). Marufubens
peaked at 1,441 at 11,460 s (1,439 at 11,470 s). And the first film's "ninety" stomach buds on
nisimocrax leaves that never grew past their first body are 85 of 149 by my count: 85 of those
budded children never had a child. The first film's "two other budded lines of ten or more (31
and 10)" are three, 31, 10 and 10.

## What I could not check

- The cause of tank 2's stop. The recorder refused a snow reading of minus infinity at
  13,700 s. A probe of that seed was running on the morning of 2026-09-25; if it finds the cause,
  scene 19's third caption ("We have not found its cause yet") should change.
- Why the eater lines faded, why the two tanks' bets split, and why selection keeps the speck of
  stomach. All three are marked as guesses on screen.
- Three verdict rows are reused from the first film's reading: S2 (stomach children's age at death), F4
  (newcomers' first snow against their column's mean) and B1 came from the round's own reader via
  the first film. The rest I read again.
- Whether the cousin shows what the captions say. Each scene restarts from a checkpoint and
  drifts. Body 3559 should run out of reserve within its 46 s scene, but the cousin may take a
  little more or less than the recorded 44.5 s. Body 48048's bud is a recorded birth that a cousin
  stepped from 22,500 s will not reproduce birth for birth. The captions say "in the run" for both.
- How the account chart behaves when its body dies mid-scene. The portrait keeps its last
  place when the body is gone (`SafariPlans.cs`); the chart's handling of a death is the visuals
  agent's code and I have not read it running.
