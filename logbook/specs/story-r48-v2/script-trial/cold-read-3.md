**2. Three most machine-sounding lines**

- "With 0.54 J a second in and 0.49 J out, its reserve creeps up." (Scene 2) — reads like a spec-sheet readout of two precise decimals rather than something spoken.
- "A new rule lands each newcomer at the richest depth for its food." (Scene 8) — the phrase "a new rule" is changelog language, not narration.
- "Its own 66 J could not cover both, so the gift paid the rest." (Scene 8) — ledger-style accounting of a mechanism ("the gift") that was never actually explained.

**3. The story, in two sentences**

In a simulated tank of water, tiny creatures — leaves that live on light and eaters that live on drifting "snow" — are born, grow, spend their savings on children, and starve when their energy runs out; this round's rules were changed to help eater lineages survive, and for a while they did, breeding faster and living longer than before, but every eater line still died out by the end. Instead, the round's real surprise came from the leaves: one leaf lineage picked up a tiny stomach as a random add-on and kept it for generations, ending as the largest stomach-carrying line in any tank — not the thriving eater the experiment hoped for, but a leaf that quietly held on to a stomach it barely uses.

One structural note beyond the two asks: the recurring header-style lines ("The life of body 201," "What one child cost," "Every eater line died out," "Tank 1: the small children won") work as documentary-style captions/ledes and read fine spoken aloud with a pause after — I did not count these as stumbles, but they cluster suspiciously with the report's other listy, spec-sheet phrasing, so a pass to vary their rhythm would help the whole film feel less like a table of contents.
