# Cold read 2 (Sonnet), of script.cold.md after the editor's round 2

Cold read of script.cold.md (20 scenes, 7 chapters), reading top to bottom as a first-time viewer.

## 1. Stumbles

| Scene | Line | Kind | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 4 | reread | "The drop at 850 s is two children for 129 J, leaving its 14 J cushion." Three numbers and an implicit reference to a graph feature ("the drop") collide in one clause; I had to re-read to work out that 129 J was spent, not earned. |
| 4 | 2 | reread | "The parent gives each child a share of its own body's worth in energy." Two stacked possessives ("its own body's worth") made me re-read to sort out whose body and whose share. |
| 5 | 4 | jump | "Each scene is re-run from a saved moment, and plays out as it did." A production technique is dropped into the middle of the round's premise with no setup — I don't know what a "saved moment" is or why it matters to the story. |
| 7 | 2 | number | "This one, Thallus legatribens, has 217." Never says 217 of what — individuals alive now, or every descendant ever born? The film never settles this, and it matters later. |
| 8 | 9 | reread | "That cost 99 J, so without the gift it could not have done both." Requires working the 66+72 vs. 99 arithmetic backward in your head to see why the gift mattered — too much math to do while the scene keeps moving. |
| 9 | 1 | jump | "its line, Gastrella cidutis, has 24." Same unlabeled count as Scene 7, and by Scene 14 a line's count is shown falling ("peaked... then fell to 2") — I can't tell if "has N" ever means "currently alive" versus "ever existed," and the film uses it both ways. |
| 10 | 5 | jump | "A joint lets two parts swing, driven by the body's small brain." The brain is introduced once, in a single throwaway sentence, and never mentioned again — a dangling idea that goes nowhere. |
| 17 | 1 | number | "more than twice the average age." The average age itself is never given a number anywhere in the film, so this comparison has nothing to anchor to. |

## 2. Three most machine-sounding lines

- **"Right now it earns 0.54 J a second and spends 0.49 J."** (Scene 2) — a bare earn/spend readout, no framing, like a log line.
- **"It eats 1.25 J a second, and pays 0.50 J of upkeep."** (Scene 8) — the exact same template as above with new numbers dropped in, which is what exposes it as a template rather than observation.
- **"It pays 0.73 J a second. Body 3318, young, paid 0.50 J."** (Scene 11) — a third repetition of the identical earn/spend formula; by the third time it reads as a filled-in report field, not something a narrator would say.

## 3. The story, in two sentences

In a simulated tank of water, tiny creatures called leaves (which live on sunlight) and eaters (which live on drifting food) are born, breed, mutate and starve under their own private energy budgets, while the filmmakers watch to see whether a lineage of eaters can finally survive to the end of a run. It cannot: every eater line dies out despite breeding well at first, and the round's best result is a consolation one — a lineage of leaves that picked up a small stomach and kept it, even though the stomach barely does anything for them.

Note: I did not verify any of these numbers or claims against the simulation itself — I read only this file, as instructed, and reported what a viewer would experience on a single pass.
