## The answer in one paragraph

Grazing has not arisen in 14 rounds of a world that allows it, and the reason is not the price: at round 58's prices a 0.4 m consumer cube with a capped claw and mouth costs 0.58 W and one whole-leaf kill repays 82 J or more, so one kill every two minutes keeps it alive (C). What stops it is structural (R, C, with one inference): attack is drawn at zero on every founder, mutates at 0.005 per node per birth in steps half of which clamp to zero, and can only sit on structural, link or consumer nodes, while the crowd is leaves and bladders, both capped at zero; a consumer founder is sent to the corpses on the bed (D129) while the leaves sit at −11 m, so the carrion bridge leads away from the food; and when a bite does land, the corpse appears at the victim's body point and the mouth reaches 0.87 m, while the median leaf is a 2.7 m sheet, so a killer that bit the leaf's outer half cannot eat what it killed, and the corpse sinks away at 0.05 m/s. Round 45's one killer clade made 62 corpses and touched none (R, logbook 0114). I therefore recommend: do not start in the nursery. Start with D134's own rule applied to the world: a hand-set grazer (a real round-58 plan with a consumer part at attack 1 and intake 1, a bladder to hold leaf depth) inoculated into a resumed round-58 checkpoint for 2,000 s, read for kills, kills eaten, and R0. That one run says whether the organ works in this world, which no nursery score can. If it eats, the ecology route (inoculation into round 60) is enough and the nursery is a brain refinement; if it kills but cannot eat, the reach rule is the block and is the owner's to change before any search; and the whole thing follows round 59, because the anchoring cell puts leaves where the mouths already land.

## 1. Nursery, ecology, or both?

**Recommendation: both, in that order, the ecology's positive control first.** Can grazing arise in the ecology itself? Not from this crowd without a push (M, R): seed 1 had 481 attack-carrying births out of 179,318 and 444 with intake, and zero kill rows; the living at 48,000 s carry 126 consumer nodes, all at intake 0, and no attack above 0. What stops it, ranked by my reading:

1. **Reach versus leaf span** (C, with the untouched corpses of round 45 as the only run evidence, R). `KillPart` puts the corpse at the body's point; `InReach` is corpse radius + 0.5 m = 0.87 m for a 0.21 m³ body; the median leaf's half-span is 1.36 m. The "as built" note says the body point is under a metre from the truth "at the campaign's body sizes"; it is not at round 58's leaf sizes.
2. **The two-gene valley with both genes priced from birth and the bridge pointing the wrong way** (R). Attack and intake must co-occur in one body; founders draw attack 0; `foundersFollowCorpses` is on, so consumer founders land on the bed by settled corpses (96% of corpse joules are settled, M) and the leaves are 10 to 14 m above them (M). Consumer founders in round 52 lived a median of about 110 s and none bred (R, 0127).
3. **Caps and the mutation step** (R). Leaf and bladder nodes cannot carry attack or intake, so the whole crowd is a dead end for the gene; the first mutant carries about 0.1 to 0.2 attack.
4. **Not the price** (C): see 3 below. Not contact either (M): about 2% of the living are touching another body at any physics step (116 bodies, 269 pairs, 97% held, seed 1), so a drifter among leaves does meet them.

So the nursery is not the tool for the first question. Its principle (D133, D134) is that what it breeds counts only once it out-breeds its ancestor in a round; a grazer's first question is whether the organ earns at all, and that is a world question. The positive control D134 required before any search belongs in the world here. The nursery becomes the right tool for the second question, the brain that finds leaves and stays on a kill, once the first is answered yes.

Implications: record, one screen entry and a world-rule question (reach) if it fails; rounds, no new round until the screen is read; machine, about an hour at 8 threads after round 58 ends (G, from round 58's pace of 0.5 to 1 times real time at 5,400 bodies); risk, low, a cousin resume that changes nothing recorded.

## 2. The score, if the nursery is used

**Recommendation: (a) is already (c) for a leafless body, so start there with the existing `Net` reward and no code; add an `eaten` measure only for the knockout read; reject (b).** Facts (R): the recipe nursery scores `Net` or a `WeightedSum` over start, end, net, food, light, work, upkeep, handling, exuded, path, fresh, survived, diedAt. `food` is the ledger's `FoodIncome` (snow); `WorldMouth.ApplyIntake` credits `creature.Energy` directly and never touches the ledger, so corpse joules reach the score only through `net` and `end`. A `WeightedSum` on `food` scores a grazer zero. For a body with no light tissue in a tank with no carrion, `net` = corpse joules eaten − upkeep − work, and upkeep is a constant per body, so `Net` is corpse joules up to a constant: exactly (c). (b) in a mixed tank pays scavenging, and attribution would need a killer on the corpse: `Corpse` carries `CreatureId` and no killer, the kill row in `lineage.jsonl` carries `by`, and `WorldState` writes corpses to the checkpoint, so a `KilledBy` field is a checkpoint format change. Attribution by construction is cheaper: tankSize 1, no carrion, leaves as the only other bodies, births held. One caveat (R, G): a leaf placed too deep starves inside an episode and becomes carrion the grazer did not kill; place the food leaves in the lit band and read `corpsesFromKills` against `corpses` in the tank trace.

What the nursery lacks (R): a setting to inoculate non-scored bodies. `World.Inoculate(genome, count, height)` exists and the tank already inoculates its focal bodies, so a `food.bodies` setting (a genomes file and a count, placed by Jakobi's draw in a depth band) is a nursery-side build, about a day with tests (G).

Implications: record, a recipe and its registry entry, a build spec; rounds, none; machine, minutes for the control, hours for a search (D148's 200 s a generation on 14 efficiency cores, R); risk, the tank measures a brain for leaves laid as a lawn in still water, which round 59's anchoring may make real and round 58's drifting crowd is not.

## 3. Does grazing pay at round 58's prices?

Yes on paper, by a wide margin (C; the leaf is a real living body, id 164059, the modal plan of leaf root and two bladders):

- Leaf part 0.043 m³ and 7.0 m² (1.3 × 2.7 m, 12 mm thick); health pool 0.55 at toughness 1; whole body 103 J of tissue.
- Grazer, a 0.4 m consumer cube at attack 1 and intake 1: upkeep 0.38 W + claw 0.10 W + mouth 0.10 W = 0.58 W. Blow 0.96 health/s kills the bare leaf in 2 metabolic steps (1 s); the leaf is the root, so the whole plant is the corpse. Mouth 96 J/s empties it in about a second if in reach.
- Yield: 103 J + the reserve share; kept after the 0.2 waste 82 J at zero reserve, 122 J at 50 J of reserve; no handling cost, because corpse intake bypasses the ledger (R; the config's 0.1 J per J is charged only on the pool draw). Break-even: one kill every 140 s on standing cost alone.
- The arms race (C): a capped cuticle on this leaf absorbs 0.5 × 7.0 = 3.5 health/s and costs 3.5 W. No part under 3.5 m² of attacking area can then hurt it. A surface leaf lying flat can earn up to 35 W (C, a ceiling before day, season and weather), a leaf at 10 m about 6.6 W, so leaves can afford armour at the surface and not at depth: the first grazer round may breed cuticles, not grazers. Read `protectionShare` first.
- Not measured: how often a grazer actually meets a leaf. The crowd's 2% touching (M) is a leaf's number; a grazer's would come from the screen in 7 (`partsKilled`, `bodiesEaten` and the kill rows' `by` against the inoculants' ids in `pool/index.tsv`).
- The ledger screen, once `Evosim.Ledger` is rebuilt on the current source: `./scripts/ledger.ps1 -Genome scratch/grazing-fable/r58s1j-median-leaf.json -Config runs/r58-s1j/2026-10-12-080742-dfc0e881/config.json -Clearance 10 -Depth 1,5,10 -Density 1 -Exposure 2 -Protection photosynthetic=0.5` and `... -Genome scratch/grazing-fable/r58s1j-mouth.json -Attack consumer=1 -Intake consumer=1`; its Mouth section prints the claw's watts, the corpse's kept joules, steps to kill bare and armoured, and the emptying time, which should match the numbers above.

## 4. The starting bodies

No evolved body carries both genes (M, R): round 58's living have no attack; `inocula/r55-nursery/` are absorptive trunks with a link (snow eaters); `inocula/pool-r47/` are four absorptive stomachs; D133's round-52 bodies had a corpse mouth and no claw. **Recommendation: hand-set attributes on a real round-58 plan.** A genome's `attack` and `intake` are plain fields in its JSON (R), the loader refuses a value above the cell's cap, and the ledger's `--attack consumer=1` override is the same edit. `scratch/grazing-fable/r58s1j-mouth.json` is a living body of seed 1 with a leaf root, bladders, a structural part and a consumer part; set the consumer node's attack and intake to 1 and, for the control, the leaf node's cell to structural so the score is not light. A hand-built one-part consumer cube with a bladder child is the cleaner control and the ledger's own kind of body. Either way the inoculant lands with `FounderEnergyJoules` × birth fraction (R, Core CLAUDE.md), so the screen sets the purse as the nursery's `--purse` does.

## 6. One-part leaves

Round 58 has 16 one-part bodies among 5,391 (M). The crowd is 3-part plants whose root is the leaf (every sampled root is photosynthetic, M), so a bite on the leaf is a whole-plant death counted as `bodiesEaten`, and a bite on a bladder is `partsKilled` and a plant that lives on without its float. **Recommendation: accept whole-plant eating as the first rung and name it browsing, reading `bodiesEaten` and `partsKilled` apart; make grazing a plant survives the second rung, on plants with a structural or anchoring root and leaf modules.** Those plants are what round 59's anchoring cell may grow (an anchored stalk with leaves, 19% of the living already carry an indeterminate node, M), which is one more reason for the order in 7.

## 7. Sequencing against round 59

**Follow, not replace, not alongside.** The turning drag (D161) changes every swimmer's water, so a brain bred before it is bred for other water (R). The anchoring cell (D162) changes where leaves are: anchored to the bed and the reef caps, they become a lawn in the place D129 already sends mouthed founders and where 96% of corpse joules lie (M), so the scavenger's niche and the grazer's coincide; for grazing, the anchoring cell probably does more than any nursery (G). The positive-control screen fits the gap after round 58 ends and before round 59 launches, as a cousin resume of round 58's last checkpoint; it needs no round-59 code and a repeat on round 59's world is cheap.

## 8. What the coordinator missed

- Corpse intake pays no handling cost while snow eating pays 0.1 J per J (R): a pricing asymmetry in the grazer's favour the owner may not intend.
- Reach against leaf span, and the corpse sinking at 0.05 m/s (R, C): the likeliest reason a killer gains nothing in practice.
- The nursery's `food` measure excludes corpse joules (R); only `net` and `end` see them.
- `unitsEaten` is in units of 100 J (R): 218 to 286 units is 21,800 to 28,600 J over the run, not joules.
- A working grazer in a crowd where 2% of bodies touch at every step and a kill takes a second (M, C) is a massacre risk; the matter budget is closed (15,000 units), so a producer crash feeds back; the screen's `alive` is read with its kills.
- A capped cuticle makes a surface leaf unkillable by any part under 3.5 m² (C): the round may answer with armour.
- tankSize above 1 mixes kills between focal bodies (R): the grazing recipe sets 1.
- Round 45 is the precedent (R, 0114): one killer clade, jointed, 62 kills, no corpse touched, and no kill in rounds 46, 48 or 50.
