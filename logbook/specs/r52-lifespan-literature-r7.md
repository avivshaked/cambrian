## 3. Synthesis by question

*Measured* is read in a source, *inferred* is the agent's arithmetic from measured numbers, *guessed* is a judgement with no source.

**L1. Gompertz shape and the doubling fraction f.** Measured: mortality rises with age in most wild animals studied (NUS13, 175 species) but not all; JON14 found constant, decreasing and humped trajectories, and ectotherms often age negligibly (REI22, DAS22). Doubling times: humans and rhesus about 8 years (FPW90); Sacher's mouse 220 d and human 3,100 d (KIR15). Across 96 mammals the Gompertz rate does not slow with body size; longer life comes from later onset and lower prime-age mortality (PER19). Within primate genera the rate is nearly invariant and lifespan varies through early and age-independent mortality (COL21). A fitted rate is confounded with initial mortality (RS02, KIR15). Inferred: f is about 0.10 for humans and about 0.25 for the mouse (the lab median is assumed). A pure Gompertz model with median L gives, at f = 0.2, a CV of age at death of 0.34 and terminal over mean mortality of about 10.5 (0.19 and 21 at f = 0.1), inside JON14's range. Verdict: f = 0.2 is defensible as a value. The scaling is where the evidence bites: the simulator stretches the doubling time in proportion to the heritable L, so a longer-lived mutant ages more slowly per unit time. That matches the rough across-species correlation and runs against the within-lineage evidence (COL21) and PER19. A within-population lifespan gene that works mainly through the slope is the pattern real populations show least (inferred; a modelling choice to state, not a bug).

**L2. Disposable soma and the repair exponent a.** Measured: the theory needs only "some cost" (KR91; CIC97). Across species a longer life does not cost more energy per unit time per gram (SPK05), and long-lived species turn proteins over more slowly with less ATP (SWO21), the opposite sign to "longer L costs more upkeep", though between species. Within species, direct selection for long life cut fecundity (ZBH95), and 21 of 26 wild vertebrate studies support early-late trade-offs (LEM15); several studies uncouple them (MC19; COH20 "mixed"). Guessed: no source gives an exponent. a = 0.5 is a modelling judgement that guarantees an interior optimum; the strongest literature-backed statement is that a > 0 is required and its size is unknown.

**L3. Reproduction accelerating ageing (the birth push).** Measured: lifelong brood enlargement in jackdaws raised the Gompertz slope from 0.16 to 0.25 a year, left the intercept unchanged, and cut remaining life from 4.7 to 3.8 years (BBV20). The bird meta-analysis finds -0.05 log-odds of survival per egg and a flat landscape within species (WIN25); SN12 finds the cost in males only; a 10-year cricket study found none (RM19); lab costs act through short-term mortality rather than ageing (BOO14). Inferred: a per-birth clock advance repeated each breeding year appears as a slope change, the pattern BBV20 saw, so the push has the right form. Its size in the birds is about 0.04 to 0.1 doublings per adult-sized offspring. The simulator charges 1 doubling times relative size, 0.31 doublings a birth at this world's median newborn size of 0.31 (a project figure): 3 to 8 times the jackdaw figure per birth, and 10 to 25 times per adult-size equivalent. BBV20's intervals are wide.

**L4. Injury accelerating ageing (the wound push).** Measured: clipping 18% of wing area shortened bumble-bee life (CAR92, no effect size seen); one sublethal head strike cut fly median lifespan by 21%, saturating at four strikes (KAT13); human deficits accumulate about 3% a year and predict death (MIT01). Inferred: one doubling (0.2 L) shortens the median by about 0.2 L, the same order as the fly figure by coincidence of scale. Two warnings: injury effects saturate (KAT13) where the simulator's pushes are additive and unbounded, and the mechanism may be acute rather than an age advance (BOO14). Verdict: direction supported, magnitude guessed; no comparative or meta-analytic source.

**L5. Heritability and response to selection (c = 0.08, step 0.15 L).** Measured: life-history h2 about 0.26 in wild animals and 0.12 in Drosophila (MR87), below 0.10 for human longevity (RUBY18); fitness traits have a high additive CV and high residual variance (H92). Selection responds fast: late-reproduced flies doubled mean life in 21 generations at uncontrolled density only (LC85); direct selection works both ways (ZBH95); mortality regimes diverged in 50 to 90 generations (SADK00). Inferred: per-birth mutational variance c times the step squared is 0.0018 L2, a mutational CV of about 4.2% of L; against ageing's own variance (about 0.116 L2) the ratio is about 0.016, at or above the measured ceiling the earlier file quoted (unverified). With the founders' spread and h2 of 0.1 to 0.26, a response of 1 to 3% of L a generation is plausible. Verdict: c = 0.08 with a 0.15 step is fast, defensible as a deliberate speed-up for a short simulation and not as a natural value.

**L6. Models in which lifespan evolves.** Measured: Gompertz-like mortality emerges from mutation accumulation (PEN95); a programmed age of death evolves under local dispersal, while lifespan grows without bound under global mixing (TRA04); local reproduction plus resource depletion favours shorter lives (WER15); kin competition weakens selection on survival (RP10); ageing stably evolves and negative ageing does not (GT22); the end of fertility and the onset of senescence converge (ROG24, MRR19). KK16's re-implementations found the spatial "programmed death" results came from death conferring movement or a shorter generation time. Inferred: what mattered was the dispersal scale and kin competition, whether death stands in for movement, whether lifespan is bounded by a cost (TRA04: unbounded without one), and whether the mortality shape is imposed or evolved (this simulator imposes it with a fixed f). In this world, with newborns dispersed over a disc and a carrying current, shorter L could be favoured through kin competition; KK16's caution says to check whether any such result is a movement or generation-time effect.

## 4. Gaps and threats to validity

- Semantic Scholar keyword search failed on 7 of 12 attempts; discovery leaned on OpenAlex's noisy ranking, so recall is unmeasured.
- Most full texts were gated: 14 of 33 included read as PDF with page locators, 10 as PMC HTML without pages, the rest (KR91, ZBH95, CAR92, SPK05, BOO14, SN12) abstract only.
- One reader, briefed by a prior file (a confirmation risk). New to this round: COL21, PER19, KK16, WER15, KAT13, CAR92, RM19, SWO21, LC85, MR87, GT22, ROG24.
- L3 rests on birds and one lab's lifelong design (BOO14 and BBV20 not independent); L4 has no comparative source; L2 gives no exponent anywhere.
- L1 mixes captive and wild data, and a fitted rate is confounded with initial mortality.
- No evolvable-lifespan paper from an artificial-life venue turned up; L6 is theoretical biology and statistical physics.
- Verification: CrossRef and OpenAlex checked every included DOI, with no retractions; the Boonekamp 2014 addendum (results unchanged), Winder's version-of-record title change, and a PLOS self-link on Peron.
- Unverified inputs: the mouse lab median and a fly doubling time used for f; the unit conversions in L3 and L5 are the agent's arithmetic.
- Not searched: human trauma epidemiology beyond one query, plant and clonal senescence, sex differences in ageing.
