## 0. Four things I found that change the design

These came out of reading the code beside the owner's idea, and they matter more than any algorithm choice.

1. **Moving is free in this world, and so is thinking.** The episode config the nursery runs on carries `workCostMultiplier 0`, `neuralCostPerNeuronWatts 0` and `neuralCostPerConnectionWatts 0` (`scratch/nursery/runs/nursery-small/2026-09-29-222243-756e6120/config.json`), and round 53's launcher sets the same (`scratch/wt-r53/rounds/env-r53.ps1` line 63: `EVOSIM_NEURON_COST = 0; EVOSIM_CONNECTION_COST = 0; EVOSIM_WORK_COST = 0`) [K]. So a stroke costs a body nothing at the joint. What a body pays for is tissue: upkeep per cubic metre per second, and a link cell's upkeep and matter. Two consequences. The owner's first rung, "sense depletion and move anywhere", has no price on the moving side; it is paid whenever moving raises expected intake at all. And the calling agent's caveat that "the Energy sense drops when a body starts swimming" cannot hold here, since swimming changes no burn [K from the prices; the sense is `Energy / StandingWatts`, `Organism.cs` lines 617-618].

2. **The nursery's 2,000 J purse blinds the Energy sense.** The sense reads `tanh(secondsOfReserve / 3000)` (`CreatureSenses.cs` lines 172, 352-358; `RunConfig.cs` line 3045). A trickle founder in round 53 was endowed 69.7 J for 600 s of standing watts (`r53-read/read-30000.txt`, clause E1, seed 1), so a small body burns about 0.12 W [K]. At 2,000 J that is about 17,000 s of reserve, `tanh(5.7) = 0.99998`: the channel reads a constant, and its derivative, which is the one whole-body "am I gaining or losing" signal every kind of creature has, is about 1e-5 per meal [K arithmetic, B on the watts of the pooled bodies]. At the world's own founder purse (600 s of standing watts) the sense reads about 0.2 and a 5 J meal moves it by about 0.014 in a step, a usable pulse. The purse should be the world's rule, not a nursery number.

3. **The world already carries the depletion the owner wants, and one sense that reads it.** A stomach draws `density × clearance × dt` from its own 1 m cell, drew 45 to 52% of it per half-second step in round 48, sits at 5 to 25% of an untouched cell after a minute, and the water refills at 0.06 to 0.1 of the gap a second (`src/Evosim.Core/CLAUDE.md`, "A mouth is priced at the cell it is emptying") [K]. A leaf fixes `min(light, uptake × spent matter)` in its cell (`StandardCellTypes.cs` lines 51-73), and round 53's leaves were uptake-limited on 66 to 70% of steps (clause N2, all three seeds) [K]: a leaf's spot runs out too, of spent matter, not of light. Every draw is rationed in three passes so that a crowd shares a cell and a corpse in proportion (`World.cs` lines 3151-3178; `WorldMouth.cs` lines 402-478) [K]. The `Chemical` channel reads the edible density at the part through `x/(x+10)` (`CreatureSenses.cs` lines 224-234), so a stomach can read its own cell draining: at round 53's bed snow of 1.4 to 1.7 J/m³ (clause N1) a fresh cell reads 0.12 to 0.15 and a drained one 0.01 to 0.04, and a `Differentiate` neuron on it reads about −0.06 per step while the cell drains [K arithmetic]. **No channel reads the spent-matter field a leaf lives on** [B: the channel list in `NeuronInput.cs` has none, and `CreatureSenses.cs` samples `Nutrients` and `Scent` only]. A leaf can only notice depletion through Energy.

4. **A tank of several bodies is cheap and the coupling is the point.** `World.Inoculate(genome, count, heightY)` admits any number of copies at the tank's own placer (`World.cs` line 5128; no method places a body at a chosen x, z) [K]; `BirthsHeld` holds breeding for all of them (`WorldAssay.cs` line 19); and the farm's `Simulation` takes a thread count and is bit-identical at any (`Simulation.cs` line 192; `src/Evosim.Farm/CLAUDE.md`) [K]. One 300 s episode in the small tank costs 1.00 s with the current and 0.42 s still (`fast-nursery-measurements.md`, measurement 1), and a two-part body's own physics is about 0.33 s of that [B, from Fable's earlier 1 s per 90,000 steps]. So a tank of eight costs about 0.7 + 8 × 0.33 ≈ 3.3 s, 0.4 s per body-episode, two and a half times cheaper than one body per tank [G, within a factor of two]. D134 rejected several bodies in one world because a child's score would depend on its tank-mate. Under the owner's design that dependence is the selection environment, not a fault: it is what the ecology's fitness is.

## 1. The design

**The tank.** The current small tank, made by the farm's own binding from the round's launcher: round 53's rules at 100 m² by 5 m, flat bed, no reef, no trickle, matter scaled to the volume, the floor closed (`fast-nursery-measurements.md`, "The episode configs"; `Program.cs` `EpisodeConfig` lines 136-148). Nothing in it is invented except its size and its larder. Per episode, three things are drawn at random after Jakobi (178, p.8: vary every aspect the task does not need, from trial to trial; p.12: but not so much that nothing evolves): the tank's radius over 80 to 120 m², the current's phase and whether it runs at all (still water in a third of episodes), and each body's starting depth. The examination tank is the round's own depth, 25 m, at 400 m² (`env-r53.ps1` line 44 gives the round 22,000 m² by 25 m), which is D133's exam made honest for round 53's world.

**The larder, derived from the round by numbers.** Cutting a patch out of a checkpoint is not available: the farm restores a whole world (`CheckpointFidelity.cs` line 330 is the only restore path, and a checkpoint is the run's own 22,000 m²) [K], and a cut patch would be new field code with a different grid [B]. What is cheap and faithful is to read the round's densities and lay them: corpses at the round's corpses-per-square-metre and joules (round 53 settled 26,000 to 31,000 husks holding 328 to 395 kJ; clause H2), snow at the round's bed density (N1: 1.42, 1.62, 1.70 J/m³), and the light as the round's. A reader script that prints these from a run's read is a few lines. `PlaceCorpse` exists (`WorldAssay.cs` lines 44-82); a `LaySnow` beside it, booked into `EnergyIn` and `MatterInfluxedTotal` the same way, is the one new world-side method, about twenty lines, and it is assay plumbing like `PlaceCorpse`, not a rule [B]. The density is drawn per episode over half to double the round's, as D134's design A already draws the carrion count.

**Creatures and grouping.** A population of P = 48 genomes, split every generation at random into six tanks of M = 8, each body in three tank draws with different mates, so each genome swims three episodes a generation among 21 different mates. All tanks of a generation share the generation's seeds (the same corpse layout, the same current phase) so that within-generation comparisons are fair up to the mates. The pool is drawn as now from real snapshots (`PickBodies`, lines 218-301), in two kinds run as two campaigns: mouths (the pool as built, `--diet corpse`) and jointed stomachs (`--diet any` with a leaf excluded and an absorptive part required; round 53 has such lines, clause L4 in each seed). Eight bodies in 100 m² is one per 12 m², a crowd of the round's order near the bed [G].

**The evaluation.** Each body's score is its **net energy over the episode**: reserve plus tissue plus gestation at the end, less the same at the start, the world's own currency, which the nursery already logs as `Net` (`Swim`, lines 335, 412-424) [K]. Income is logged beside it, per body, from `Organism.LastLedger` summed over metabolic steps (the world's totals cannot be read per body once there are several) [K that the ledger exists per organism; B that summing it is all that is needed]. The purse is the world's founder rule, 600 s of standing watts, not 2,000 J (finding 2). Births held. The episode is 300 s, as D134 ruled.

Why net and not income. D133 chose income because with a 200 J purse every body died and net was flat; with the founder purse over 300 s a non-eater of 0.12 W ends at about 34 J of 70 and lives, so net is not flat, and it prices the body: a child that grows a bigger part pays for it. Income would let a body grow without limit once bodies evolve. Net is what gates reproduction in the ecology (`Organism.cs` line 620 onward), so it is the closest proxy to what the ecology pays [K for the gate, B for "closest"].

**The evolutionary algorithm.** The owner's, plainly: fixed population; evaluate; select parents by tournament of three on the running mean score; children by `Mutator.Mutate` at the config's own rates and sensor pool (`Mutator.cs` lines 60-130); no crossover; the top eight kept as elites and re-evaluated every generation among fresh mates, their score the running mean of every generation they have swum, as the archive's incumbents already are (`Evolve`, lines 652-665). The running mean is what tames the mate noise: an elite that was lucky in its mates is re-drawn next generation. No crossover because Core has none (no `Crossover` symbol under `src/Evosim.Core`) and the ecology breeds without one; adding an operator the world lacks would make the nursery's children a kind the world cannot make [K for the absence, B for the reason]. Lenski's populations and Covert's stepping-stone result both rest on asexual lineages with deleterious steps kept (185, p.3; 190, pp.3, 5), so elitism must not be truncation of everything below the elite: the tournament keeps the bottom breeding a little. The D134 replacement margin (two paired standard errors on common seeds, `Insert` lines 785-813) does not transfer to different mates and is replaced by the running mean.

**Bodies evolve, with the kind locked.** The nursery today grafts the child's neurons onto the parent's body (`Graft`, lines 496-507; `Child`, lines 509-522). The proposal drops the graft and mutates the whole genome, but refuses a child whose cell types changed (a bud, `cellTypeChance 0.005` in the config, or a node addition of another type), re-drawing as `Child` re-draws a node-count change today. Shape, half-extents, joint types and limits, scale, edges and neurons all move at the ecology's rates. Reason: fully free bodies in a lit tank scored on net energy would find the leaf, which is the attractor 53 rounds have shown, and would answer a question the owner has already had answered. Pilat found that steering was learned fast on bodies that hold a steady orientation and poorly on spinning ones (179, pp.5-6), and Auerbach that environment shaped bodies only once complexity carried a cost (195, p.7); here tissue is the cost, and the lock keeps the search on the kind's own bodies. A brain-only arm on fixed bodies runs beside it at the same cost, which keeps D133's clean question alive rather than giving it up.

**Examination and the way back.** Every ten generations, and at the end, each campaign's best eight are examined on held-out seeds in three ways: alone in the small tank (the sitter and the free-rider show here), among fresh mates, and in the exam tank at 25 m with the round's larder. Each is read against its knockout (the senses that matter zeroed, as now, lines 524-543) and against a brainless copy (every sensor input a constant, a new option) and its ancestor, on net energy and on the behaviour numbers (distance to the nearest food, share of steps in a fresh cell, distance travelled). Winners and knockouts go out as one-line genomes for `EVOSIM_INOCULATE` (DESIGN §8.6, `scratch/wt-nursery/DESIGN.md` line 2552), and the transfer test is Fable's earlier one: each winner and its knockout inoculated in one seed of the next round, read on income and persistence in `lineage.jsonl` and `absorptive.jsonl`. Whether a round carries them stays the owner's, as D133 left it.

## 2. Is the first rung paid, and for whom

The rung, in this world's terms: gate the stroke on whether the spot is still feeding you. Since the stroke is free (finding 1), the question is only whether moving raises expected intake.

- **A stomach (absorptive, jointed).** Paid, and strongly [B]. Its cell falls to 5 to 25% of fresh within a minute and refills at 0.06 to 0.1 of the gap a second; a body one metre away in fresh water earns four to twenty times more. It has the sense (`Chemical`, and `Differentiate` on it) and needs one neuron between the sense and the oscillator. This is the cleanest test of the owner's idea and I would run it first. What it does not have is a direction: it moves anywhere, and Berg and Brown's bacterium does no more (186, p.4: no steering, the post-tumble direction independent of the gradient), so "anywhere" is the biological rung, not a shortcut.
- **A mouth (consumer, jointed).** Half paid [B]. A corpse in reach is emptied in a step or two (measurement 4), so the spot runs out at once and "move anywhere" is what the pooled bodies already do all episode (the calling agent's reading of the ancestors, which I take as their observation). The rung that is left for a mouth is the pause, not the stroke: rest while a corpse is in reach. With intake so fast that is worth a step or two an episode, almost nothing. The paid rung for a mouth is the run-and-tumble on scent (option 1), which needs the same `Differentiate` and one more neuron: a joint offset only while the scent falls.
- **A leaf.** Unpaid as the world stands, twice over [B]. It has no sense of the spent matter that limits it (finding 3), and it moves only by carrying a link, which pays upkeep, catches no light from round 51 (`HANDOFF.md`, "links stop catching light from round 51"), and is what round 53 selected against: the jointed share of seed 1's holding line fell from 7.9% to 1.6% (clause O4). The calling agent read that fall as "moving costs a leaf more than it earns"; work is free, so the cost is the link's tissue, and the reading is unsupported until `scripts/ledger.ps1` says what a link costs a leaf against what a fresh cell earns it. That screen is seconds. Giving a leaf a sense of its field would be a world rule, the owner's.

**How to find out cheaply.** Two hand-wired brains, each a small grid, each paired episode by episode against its own knockout as measurement 3 was, each read on net energy and on a behaviour number:
- *The stomach shuffle*: oscillator times a gate that opens when `Differentiate(Chemical)` is below a threshold or `Chemical` is below a level, on ten jointed stomach bodies in a tank laid with the round's snow. Behaviour number: share of steps in a cell above half of fresh.
- *Run-and-tumble on scent*: the oscillator plus a joint offset while `Differentiate(Scent)` is negative, on the ten pooled mouths, with the carrion fine and the corpses laid at the bed. Behaviour number: distance to the nearest corpse, and the count reached. Berg and Brown's drift is a seventh of the swimming speed (186, pp.1, 4), so this needs the 32 episodes a body that measurement 3 used, and 64 would be safer.

Costs are in section 5. If the stomach shuffle beats its knockout and the scent control does not, the owner has learned that the world pays the rung for one kind and the mouth's path runs through a slower larder or a slower mouth, which is the feeding-change proposal HANDOFF already queues.

## 3. The calling agent's proposal, weighed

Right: the nursery is already world-derived in its rules, physics, mutation and currency, and the differences are exactly the four it names [K from `Program.cs`]; net energy is the one score that serves every kind; several bodies in one tank are needed for depletion and competition and halve the water's cost; Jakobi's per-episode draws; the controls list; letting the body evolve covers option 3; and it is honest that D133's clean question is diluted (I keep it as an arm rather than give it up).

Wrong or unsupported, in order of consequence:
1. "The Energy sense drops when a body starts swimming": work is priced at 0, and at the nursery's purse the sense is saturated anyway (findings 1 and 2). The purse is the fix, and it is a bigger change than any it proposed.
2. "Moving costs a leaf more than it earns": the price of moving is zero; the fall in jointed leaves is a link's tissue price or dilution by a crowd that doubled (7,403 alive against 3,431), and the ledger can say which in seconds.
3. "Seed the tank from a patch cut from a checkpoint": no such path exists, and the round's densities read from its own clauses are the cheaper and equally faithful seed.
4. "Test each creature in several tanks with random neighbours and average": right in spirit, but the population should be its own neighbours, reshuffled each generation, with elites re-evaluated on a running mean; that is what makes the mates a selection environment rather than noise to be averaged away, and it is what Bejjani's agents met (192, p.5, no objective, single-parent mutation).
5. It did not say that the world already rations a shared cell and a shared corpse in proportion (finding 3), which is the mechanism the owner's design rests on, nor that no channel reads a leaf's limiting field.
6. Net energy at 300 s with 2,000 J would have been fine arithmetically but blind at the sense; with the founder purse both hold.

## 4. Against the four options, and against the code

The stomach shuffle is the same control for stomachs, and it is the owner's rung directly.
- **Option 2 (search without a working control)** stays weak (Lehman and Miikkulainen 189, p.6; Lenski 185, p.5), but the search is now so cheap (section 5) that running it beside the controls costs an hour, and a null with a working control is readable.
- **Option 3 (bodies that can steer)** is absorbed: bodies evolve with the kind locked, and the pool can prefer bodies with two scent-sensing parts, which the `Scent` channel makes directional per part (`NeuronInput.cs` lines 191-201) [K].
- **Option 4 (credit for nearness)** is not needed. The owner's rung is paid in the currency for stomachs, and every physics success in round 7 that paid for approach (Chaumont 184, pp.5-6, 11-12; Pilat 179, p.4) also had a human choose founders or a fixed body; Woolley shows the price of an objective that is not the thing wanted (181, pp.5, 7).

## 5. Controls, the risks of selecting the wrong thing, and what to measure first

**Controls.** Knockout (the sense in question zeroed), brainless (every sense zeroed), ancestor, held-out seeds, held-out mates, alone in the tank, the exam at the round's depth; the two hand-wired positive controls; and the brain-only arm on fixed bodies as the measurement D133 asked for.

**What could be selected instead, and the guard for each.**
- *The sitter.* Corpses settle within 30 s (Fable's proposal, §1); a body that sinks scores. Guard: lay corpses on the bed from the start and draw the start depth; read the winner alone in still water and see whether it moves at all.
- *The free-rider.* A body that eats only from a mate's corpse share. The proportional split caps it at its share (`WorldMouth.cs` lines 449-451); the alone-in-tank reading exposes it.
- *The lucky draw of mates.* Guard: running means over re-evaluated elites; three tank draws a generation.
- *The tank's own gyre.* Guard: the Jakobi draws; the exam tank.
- *A body that grows instead of foraging* (a bigger stomach beats a moving one). Net energy prices it; the kind lock stops the leaf; and if a bigger stomach wins that is the world's answer, not a fault.
- *A purse effect.* The founder purse means a body that eats nothing ends alive; if the owner keeps 2,000 J, the Energy channel is a constant and any brain using it is selected on noise.
- *Too much variation* (Jakobi, 178, p.12) or *too little density* (Swain, 191, p.7: perception evolves only above a food-density threshold; Gomez, 177, p.6: raise the density of relevant experience). Guard: the larder drawn about the round's numbers, half to double, not lower.

**What to measure first, with costs.** The machine is on hold for the render; nothing below starts until the owner lifts it, and each is capped at four threads while the owner works (HANDOFF's 16-of-32 cap, and at most one farm-sized job beside it).
1. *The purse arithmetic*: the standing watts of the ten pooled bodies, printed by `--time-one`. One thread, a minute. Confirms finding 2 on the real bodies.
2. *A tank of eight, timed*: `--time-one` with eight inoculants in the small tank, with and without the current. One thread, two minutes. Replaces my 3.3 s guess.
3. *The ledger screen for a jointed leaf*: `scripts/ledger.ps1` on a link's upkeep against a fresh cell's uptake. Seconds. Settles the jointed-leaf reading.
4. *The stomach shuffle control*: three gates × two waters × ten bodies × 32 paired episodes × 2 brains, about 4,000 CPU-s at 1 s an episode; about 25 minutes on four threads at the 0.68 efficiency measured.
5. *The scent run-and-tumble control*: the same shape at 64 episodes a body, about 50 minutes on four threads.
6. *The pilot search*, one kind: P = 48, M = 8, three draws, 100 generations, about 60 CPU-s a generation, so about 40 minutes on four threads, plus the exam (8 winners × 3 brains × 16 episodes × 2.7 s at 400 m² by 10 m, more at 25 m) about 10 to 20 minutes. Twelve threads after the hold cuts all of it by three.
7. *The round-53 pictures* the theatre owes are unaffected and should come first, as the queue says.
