# Round 59 anchoring cell literature, pass 1: candidate pool

This is pass 1 only: a pool to choose from, not a review. The searches ran on 2026-10-10, and the search log is `search-log.md` beside this file. Every entry below came from a search or database result in this session. Nothing was downloaded, and no full text was read. **These are candidates, not papers read, and no finding is attributed beyond what an abstract states.**

**How to read the columns.**

- **Source of the line**: *abstract* means the abstract was read in this session, through Europe PMC, CrossRef or the OpenAlex abstract index. The text is kept in `verify.json`. *Search summary* means the abstract was not opened, and its content is known only from the web search tool's summary, which is weaker. *Title only* means nothing beyond the bibliographic record was seen.
- **Verified in**: CR is CrossRef, S2 is Semantic Scholar, EPMC is Europe PMC and OA is OpenAlex. Each entry matched on title, first author and year in every database listed. None carries a retraction notice in CrossRef or a retraction flag in OpenAlex.
- **Open access**: what the databases flag. *Yes* means Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex flags it open. *Mixed* means they disagree. *No* means neither flags it. The flag was not tested by fetching the text.
- **Peer-reviewed**: *yes* means a journal article or an Annual Review. A book-series review chapter is marked *yes (review series)*.
- **Age**: entries older than 25 years (before 2001) are marked *foundational*. Each is either the origin of a method later papers use or the only source found for its point.
- The "Why a candidate" text after the abstract line is my inference about how the paper bears on the anchoring cell. It is not a claim the paper makes.

The pool has 32 entries, slightly above the 30 the brief asked for. Five in-range entries could go first if the pool must be cut: Carrington 2002 ICB (C10, which Carrington et al. 2015 largely subsumes), Young 1985 (C13), Wahl 1985 (C31), Koehl 1977 (C32) and Widdows et al. 2002 (C28).

## Strongest for the pricing questions (Q1 and Q2)

*This ranking is my reading of the abstracts, not a finding.*

- **Q1, the cost:** C1 (Roberts et al. 2021) is the anchor. It is the one paper found that gives a price per unit of attachment (about 1 J per thread) and a share of the budget at a low rebuilding rate (2–8%) and a high one (up to 47%). C3 (Hawkins & Bayne 1985) gives byssus's share of carbon and nitrogen production. C6, C14 and C2 bear on whether the grip should weaken, or stay paid for, when the body is short of energy. C4 and C7 tie attachment to forgone reproduction.
- **Q2, strength against size:** C15 (Denny et al. 1985) is the theory. Velocity forces scale with structural strength, and acceleration forces outgrow it. C6 measures how force and tenacity scale with mussel size (2.8× the force for 6× the area). C20 gives a cross-species break force against thallus size for algae. C24 and C25 give per-area tenacities (MPa) and a per-unit force for tube feet. C19 and C17 test a break-when-load-exceeds-strength rule in the field.

## Where the evidence looked thin or absent

- **Costs outside mussels.** No paper in the pool prices barnacle cement, a macroalgal holdfast, an anemone's pedal disc or a tube foot in energy. Query Q1b on barnacle cement cost returned genomics and antifouling work. Q1d on holdfast allocation returned holdfast fauna. The only non-mussel costs are for gastropod mucus (C11 and C12).
- **A cost per second held.** Nothing found measures a running cost of staying attached, as opposed to building or rebuilding the structure. *Inference:* the evidence supports a build or re-attach cost and a standing upkeep (thread decay and replacement) better than a per-second holding fee.
- **Anemone release and re-attachment.** No timings or costs were found. Queries Q4b, Q4e and E4 returned almost nothing relevant. The web search named older behavioural papers (McFarlane 1969 on *Calliactis*; Ellis, Ross & Sutton 1969 on *Stomphia*) that were neither verified nor carried.
- **Sea star and urchin release costs.** The tube-foot papers give forces, not energy. The footprint left at each release is a known material loss, but no paper pricing it was found.
- **Scaling with volume.** Every scaling result found relates force to area, thread number or thread thickness. None relates it to the volume of the attachment organ. *Inference:* a breaking force that grows with volume is the design's choice, not something the literature states. Reading C15's argument, also my inference rather than its abstract's words, strength that keeps pace with drag would scale with area (length squared), not volume.
- **Searches that failed.** Three OpenAlex queries returned HTTP 429. Grey literature and Google Scholar were not searched.
