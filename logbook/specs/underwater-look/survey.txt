Two findings from reading the code (not yet seen on screen):

1. The sun in the ceiling and the key light can point different ways. TheatreSkin.Aim
   (TheatreSkin.cs:212-218) turns the key behind every camera, but PushWater, which takes the
   sun from the key (ResolveSun, 632-673), runs only from Apply, Relight and Dress (524, 556,
   800). The window's sun disc, the caustics' sideways shift and the shafts keep the bearing the
   key had when the world was dressed. The light on bodies follows each camera.

2. Light does not fall with depth on a body. The key and the ambient are the same at 1 m and
   50 m; only caustics (TheatreWater.hlsl:341-344) and the ceiling's brightness
   (TheatreSurface.shader:225-227) fade. The theatre reads no part of the run's light config.
   Round 57's config has attenuationDepth 6 m; the theatre's light reach is 18 m.

Also: the SSAO renderer feature (unity/Assets/Rendering/UniversalRenderer.asset:68-79) is built
and no theatre shader samples it (no _SCREEN_SPACE_OCCLUSION anywhere under Assets/Theatre).

What the run records that a view of the world above could be honest to: config.json "light"
(surfaceIrradiance, attenuationDepth, dayLengthSeconds, dayNightAmplitude, season*, weather*),
stats.jsonl "dayFactor" per sample (Row.cs:719 on round-57), and LightModel.DayFactorAt /
WeatherFactorAt as pure functions of t. Irradiance depends on height only, so the world has no
sun direction; at amplitude 0.5 the day factor never reaches zero, so the world has no night.
