# Where the farm's body physics spends its time: Fable's read of 2026-10-02

The owner suggested putting this question to the same Fable subagent that had read the snow's advection (`snow-advection-speed-read.md`). The subagent read main's `src/` against `cpu-profile-2026-09-26.txt` (round 49 at 16 threads, about 8,000 bodies), read-only. Nothing was edited, built or run. Its report is below, saved as it came on 2026-10-02 in the evening. Figures it marks *est.* are estimates from the code and the rows. The subagent's text keeps its own voice and has not been restyled.

The session checked two claims before relying on them. `Water.Sample` calls `CurrentField.VelocityAndAccelerationAt` on main (Water.cs:81), so the shared fade is in. And `ContactBed.Push` samples the bed's height and gradient before the clearance test that returns zero (ContactBed.cs:66-77, where the subagent's line numbers were off by about twenty), so P3's premise holds.

## 0. Which build, and what the profile already bought

The profile (`logbook/specs/cpu-profile-2026-09-26.txt`) was taken on `wt-speed`'s build; its §4 says the two changes it led to — the reef fade shared between velocity and acceleration, and the contact grid's sort partitioned by owner — were merged into a main-based build and checked identical. Main has both: `CurrentField.VelocityAndAccelerationAt` (CurrentField.cs:1093-1106) is what `Water.Sample` calls (Water.cs:79-88), and `ContactGrid` carries the partition arrays (Contacts.cs:320-327). So of the profile's shares, the 5.0% `ReefGeometry.Fade` is now paid once (about 2.5% recovered) and the grid phase is about 40% cheaper; the rest of the profile stands. No later change to the water path is recorded in HANDOFF (item 7 names this profile as the open work).

The step's shape (DynamicsWorld.cs:111-170): grid build (`BuildLinks`, four `Slabs.Run` plus `Fill`'s two) → water pass (one `Parallel.For` under a pin) → bodies (`Parallel.For(StepOne)`) → commit (`Slabs.Run`) → after-step (serial census and digest). Round 49's rows put these at 17-19 / 26-30 / 36-41 / 8-12 / 3-7% of the physics. One observation the profile does not make: that is **eight to ten fork-joins per 2-4 ms physics step**, and the commit phase, which copies a few hundred bytes per body (Creature.cs:554-567), cannot cost 8-12% of a step by its arithmetic — its cost is the fork-join plus cross-core cache traffic on arrays another thread just wrote (inference from the code; the rows measure only the phase).

**Totals (est.).** A link far from rock and shore: ~2,000 flops, **28 transcendentals** (24 of them the bed's), 4-6 sqrt. A link within a reef's fade: ~3,400 flops, 39-46 transcendentals, 8-12 sqrt. At roughly 20 ns a `Math.Sin/Cos`, 5 ns a sqrt or divide and ~0.35 ns a flop in these dependency chains, that is ~1.3 µs far and ~2.1 µs near on one thread; the rows (water 26-30% of 358 ms physics per metabolic step at ~10,000 bodies, 50 physics steps, 16 threads) imply ~1 µs per link-sample, so the estimate is the right order. **By this reading the single largest item in a far link's water sample is the bed's twelve-mode Hessian — about 40% of it — and the profile cannot say so because it did not break `BedShape.HeightGradientAndHessian` out of `ReefStreamsAt`.** The velocity's 9.1% against the acceleration's 12.1%, when the acceleration does ~3× the velocity's arithmetic, is consistent with the velocity call carrying the bed's miss (inference). The first thing to do with a free machine is re-run `scripts/profile-farm.ps1` and read that one frame.

## 3. Recommendation

**Do first, no ruling needed:** P1, P2 and P3 together (all BI, all small-to-medium, together **~10-18%** of the physics at a full crowd by these estimates), checked the way the profile's §4 checked its changes — a resume of a round against its own rows and checkpoint, digests at 1 and 16 threads, and the pace alternated in one sitting. Then P4-P6 on the water (~3%), and P7 only after measuring the sort's share. Before any of the water work, re-run `scripts/profile-farm.ps1` on a free machine with `BedShape.HeightGradientAndHessian` and `StreamsUnitWithGradient` read as their own frames: if the bed is the ~40% of a far link's sample this reading says, the owner's question is N2 and nothing bit-identical reaches it.

**Put to the owner, each in full:** N1 (one grouping of the clock and the velocity off the gradient, ~5% for a new realisation), N2 (a cheaper floor, ~7-12%, a change to world geometry), and R2 (the acceleration's cadence) — and the standing one, N3, which is the only route to a multiple rather than a percentage.

**The card.** Not the better home for a bit-identical water sample. The snow's transport matched the CPU on the card because its per-edge arithmetic has no transcendental in it — the instant's 108 sines and cosines are computed on the CPU and uploaded (`CopyStreamsInstant`, 4461-4476; the kernel multiplies with `mul.rn` to defeat FMA contraction, `src/Evosim.Farm.Gpu/CLAUDE.md`). A link's water sample has 28-46 `Math.Sin/Cos/Atan2` per link that are functions of the link's own position, and the card's `sin`/`cos` (ILGPU.Algorithms, libdevice) are not the UCRT's bits; a bit-identical kernel would need the CPU's transcendentals transcribed, which is not on. The card's home for the water is the one it already has: `GpuWorld` samples the streams, reefs and bed on the device as part of the single-precision solver (GpuWorld.cs:196-216) — N3, the owner's. For the bit-identical work, the CPU is the home.
