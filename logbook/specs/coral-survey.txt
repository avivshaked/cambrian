======================================================================================================
SUMMARY
======================================================================================================
- A reef today is one analytic mushroom: a lobed slab cap joined to a vertical cylinder stem by a smooth
  minimum. Every system reads the same signed-distance function. Placement draws reefs uniformly over the
  disc until the caps cover ReefCover of the surface, and it knows nothing about the islands.
- Round 60 ran 53 reefs at cover 0.25 (about 22,000 m2 of caps), against islands of about 8,400-9,800 m2.
  If the corals are put on the islands at today's cover, they would roof every oasis twice over, and the
  ground under a cap gets no light at all (CapTransmission 0). So coral cover on the islands has to be set
  as a share of each island, not of the tank.
- Every query on the CPU and the card loops over ALL reefs: two passes of lower bounds in the contact, and
  reach checks twice per link per step in the water, because the card does not share the fade between
  velocity and acceleration. At 53 reefs this is cheap. At hundreds or thousands of pieces it is not
  (estimate). A static grid over the reef pieces is the mitigation every richer option needs.
- The costliest single piece is the lobed cap's Atan2. The card has no instruction for it, and ILGPU runs a
  24-rotation software CORDIC (card-audit-r57.md). Any new build is a new realisation anyway, so it is the
  time to drop Atan2 (inference).
- Holdfasts already exist: the AnchoringCell (D162/D165) grips the bed or a reef on contact. D171 parked it
  after a null: weightless grazers hovered 0.27 m above the cap tops, in water that the reef's fade makes
  about 40 times slower, and starved. A coral meant for holdfasts has to fix both: bodies must touch rock,
  and water must reach the rock.
- The theatre draws the reefs as lathe meshes, displaced only inward. The shaft shader keeps at most 32
  cap discs, so round 60's 53 reefs already exceed it. Any richer shape needs a new mesh path and a baked
  shade map.

======================================================================================================
1. HOW A REEF IS DEFINED AND PLACED
======================================================================================================
Shape (ReefGeometry, Evosim.Core)
- One reef is a vertical axis at (x, z). The cap's outline is r(t) = r0(1 + sum over k=2..4 of
  a_k cos(k t + phi_k)). The cap is a slab of thickness t, rounded at the rim by t/2. The stem is a
  cylinder of radius StemRadius, and a smooth minimum with fillet t/2 joins the two. Stem 0 is a floating
  island. WB src/Evosim.Core/Environment/ReefGeometry.cs:14-22.
- The distance is first-order (exact for a circle), and its gradient and Hessian are exact derivatives of
  that function. The streams need the Hessian. The function is C1 outside the rock and C2 except on q = 0
  and the stem-top plane. ReefGeometry.cs:25-46.
- The rock is the union of the reefs: the distance is the minimum, the light is 0 under any cap, and the
  fade is the product of every reef's fade. ReefGeometry.cs:49-53.
- Per reef: X, Z, CapRadius, CapDepth, StemRadius, A2-A4, P2-P4, NoiseSeed (the theatre's only).
  ReefGeometry.cs:82-107.
- Derived per reef: capMidY, outerMax, cosMin and reach, by sampling the outline at 2,048 angles.
  ReefGeometry.cs:159-201.
- Constants: 2 m glass clearance (:63), 2 m water under the cap (:70), shallowest stemmed cap 0.5 m (:73),
  outline swing 0.4 (:76).
- Refusals: ReefGeometry.Refuse, :383-522. A reef is refused in a box (:416) and outside a grid field (:425).

Tunables (RunConfig, group "reef")
- ReefCover, 0 = off. WB src/Evosim.Core/RunConfig.cs:2016-2027.
- ReefMaxCount, default 64. :2033-2042.
- ReefCapRadiusMin/Max, defaults 6 and 16 m. :2049-2063.
- ReefOutlineRoughness, default 0.15. :2074-2084.
- ReefCapDepthMetres, default 3. :2090-2096.
- ReefCapDepthJitterMetres, default 1. :2104-2110.
- ReefCapThicknessMetres, default 2. :2117-2123.
- ReefStemRadiusFraction, default 0.25. :2131-2140.
- ReefFadeMetres, default 8. :2156-2162.
- The group remark says a config written before the group is refused (§9): RunConfig.cs:2000-2005.
- Round 60's values: cover 0.25, max count 256, the rest at their defaults
  (M runs/r60-s6001/2026-10-16-002025-a44977ba/config.json:262-273).
- Round 60's header reads "reefs 53 cover 0.25 (0.251 got)", in a tank of r = 167.37 m, depth 25 m, bed tilt
  41 m, shelves at 6/12/24 m covering 1.2/12.4/46.9% (M runs/r60-s6001.md).

The placer (ReefGeometry.Place, ReefGeometry.cs:559-685)
- It counts cover on the nutrient grid's columns (FieldCellMetres, 1 m in round 60) inside the glass.
  :571-591.
- It draws reefs until the covered columns reach the target. More reefs than ReefMaxCount is a refusal.
  :598-609.
- The shape is drawn first, in Draw: radius, depth, three amplitudes, three phases, the noise seed. :688-723.
- The centre is uniform over the disc whose outline stays 2 m inside the glass, with up to 4,000 tries, until
  DeepEnough finds depth + t + 2 m of water at the centre and at 8 outline points. :613-653, :735-749.
- The last reef is kept only if it lands nearer the target. :674-679.

The reef stream
- World.ReefPlacementIndex = ulong.MaxValue - 9, a stream of its own, drawn only by a world with reefs.
  WB src/Evosim.Core/Ecosystem/World.cs:230-234.
- Built at World.cs:1670-1677, after the bed and before the fields.
- On R61 (D192) the stream is seeded from the landscape seed, which also draws the bed, the islands and the
  current: R61 src/Evosim.Core/Ecosystem/World.cs:1666-1670 and :1687-1690, with the island map at :1879-1880.
- The manifest records every reef's parameters: WB src/Evosim.Farm/Manifest.cs:152-161.

Does placement know the islands? NO.
- Reefs are placed at World.cs:1674-1677, before the matter grid exists (:1793) and before the island map is
  made (:1866-1871).
- Place's only inputs are the config, the tank radius, the bed and the stream seed (ReefGeometry.cs:559-560).
- Round 60's reef placement and islands are therefore independent. Inference: the expected share of reef
  footprint over islands is about the islands' share of the tank, roughly 0.10.

How the islands are made (needed in order to tie the reefs to them)
- The map is GradientNoise(SeedFor(seed or landscape, IslandMapIndex), wavelength).Fbm, a pure function of
  x and z. WB World.cs:1866-1871; R61 World.cs:1879-1880.
- The threshold is the (1 - cover) quantile of the map over the live columns of the MATTER grid
  (FieldMatterCellMetres, 5 m in round 60). Columns nearer the glass than MatterIslandGlassMarginMetres are
  left out first (D185). WB src/Evosim.Core/Environment/GridField.cs:1495-1570.
- Tunables: wavelength (RunConfig.cs:1486), cover 0.1 by default (:1504), depth (:1530), glass margin
  (:1563). Round 60: 90 m, 0.05, 12 m, 30 m (round 60 header; M rounds/r60.ps1 is in WB rounds/r60.ps1:169-170).
- As measured from round 60's 100 s dump: 391 of 3,521 water columns (0.11) in 8 islands (seed 6001), and
  337 columns (0.10) in 3 islands of about 110 columns each (seed 6002). In 5 m columns that is about
  9,800 m2 and 8,400 m2. A 2,750 m2 island has an equivalent radius of about 30 m, which matches D185's
  "about one island's radius, 30 m". Source: M scratch/r60-read/s6001-s6002.txt:5,18.
  Caveat: the same read puts the nearest island column 11.5 m and 20.2 m from the glass despite the 30 m
  margin. Inference: the reader counts the 100 s dump's stock, which has already spread, and not the plan.
  Check that before using these areas.

3. THE CARD KERNEL'S REEF PATH, AND THE CPU PATH
======================================================================================================
Card (WB src/Evosim.Farm.Gpu/gpu-kernel.template)
- Memory: each reef is 16 Reals in SWorld.WR, starting at cfg.ReefAt (ReefStride = 16, :488). The order is
  X, Z, CapRadius, CapDepth, StemRadius, A2-A4, P2-P4, Round, capMidY, outerMax, cosMin, reach (:3525-3528).
- The scalars are cfg.ReefCount, HalfThickness, Fillet and ReefFade (:210, :224, :236).
- The host packs WR from ReefGeometry's private arrays by reflection: WB src/Evosim.Farm.Gpu/GpuWorld.cs:311-340.
- No ceiling on the count: ReefCount is a plain int, and WR is sized by the host (GpuWorld.cs:312-316).
  The farm's limit is ReefMaxCount. Each reef is 16 Reals: 53 reefs are about 850 Reals; 2,000 would be
  32,000 (estimate, trivial memory).
- ReefLowerBound: :3531-3547. ReefOf: :3551-3570. RoundCap and Cap: :3572 on. Outline, with 6 SinR/CosR:
  :3678-3690. Stem: :3692.
- ReefSignedDistance: :3774-3815. Two full loops over all reefs: the first finds the least bound, with an
  early exit when it is at or past the limit; the second evaluates each reef whose bound is under the best.
  Ties keep the lower index.
- ReefReaches (horizontal reach, then LB): :3819-3827. ReefFade (product over reaching reefs, with gradient
  and Hessian): :3833 on.
- Callers:
  - VelocityAt (:2169-2180) at :2139, and AccelerationAt (:2636-2650) at :2156, both in the water pass.
    Each calls ReefFade separately, so the card computes the fade TWICE per link per step. The CPU computes
    it once (CurrentField.cs:1083-1101).
  - AgainstTheWorld (:5312, reef block :5372-5400), called from the per-body (:5033) and per-link
    ContactLinks (:5159, :5266) paths.
- The whole loop is a straight scan over all N. There is no spatial structure.
- Measured context: the water pass dominates the card's passes (0.95 of 1.12 ms joined). A water thread
  spends 278-337 kcycles on the current's velocity and 154-217 on its acceleration. The slow path is MathF.Atan2,
  compiled to a software CORDIC of 24 rotations "for every reef in reach of the link". Its share is NOT
  measured. Source: M scratch/wt-speed/logbook/specs/card-audit-r57.md:862-882; M HANDOFF.md:414-418
  ("removing it would change Core's reef arithmetic and so every seed's realisation").
- Card rules any new reef code must follow (M src/Evosim.Farm.Gpu/CLAUDE.md):
  - every product written as mul.rn for bit identity (:21-26);
  - double trig through the guarded SinR/CosR (:78-88);
  - no Atan2 instruction on the card (card-audit above);
  - the default ILGPU optimisation level only (:89-93);
  - the card runs in single (:16-17).
- The transport's reef fade is a baked float per edge on the card too (GpuTransport.cs:64, 147, 153, 445),
  so it costs nothing per step.

CPU (WB src/Evosim.Dynamics)
- ContactReef.Push calls ReefGeometry.SignedDistance(limit = radius). ContactReef.cs:37-59.
- It is called from Contacts.cs:912-925 (body) and :1134-1146 (per part), with the bed's spring-damper and
  friction.
- SolverConfigWorld.cs:43-48 and :167-169 hand World.Reefs to the solver.
- Water.cs:76-90 uses CurrentField.VelocityAndAccelerationAt, with the fade shared.
- The placement path is PlacementFloor.ClearOfReefs (:99-118) and the divergence guard (Divergence.cs:150-162).
- Determinism: a pure function of the committed sphere and the rock, allocation-free (ContactReef.cs:26-29).
  The minimum is exact in any order, and ties keep the lower index (ReefGeometry.cs:789-793). The fade is a
  product IN INDEX ORDER (ReefGeometry.cs:1141-1186), so any culling structure must keep ascending order to
  keep the bits (inference).
- Wasted work today: ReefOf always computes the Hessian, even for the contact, which needs only S and the
  gradient (ReefGeometry.cs:868-886; kernel :3551). A gradient-only path for the contact would save it.
  Inference: it is bit-neutral if S and G are computed by the same expressions.

4. THE BROAD PHASE FOR BODY CONTACTS, AND WHETHER REEFS COULD SHARE IT
======================================================================================================
- CPU: ContactGrid is a uniform hash grid over bodies' bounding spheres, rebuilt every step and stored as
  compressed rows. Every sphere is entered in every cell it covers. The cell is 2 x the MEAN radius, at least
  0.25 m. WB src/Evosim.Dynamics/Contacts.cs:6-52, rule at :120 (and :499 per part). Candidates are sorted
  ascending.
- Card:
  - CandCap = 256 candidates per body per step (gpu-kernel.template:484). The gather and sort are at
    :4850-4900.
  - OverCap caps overlaps and held ids (:998, :1007, :4924, :4945).
  - EntryCap caps cell entries, starting at 1024 and growing to at least 16 x rows
    (gpu-runner.template:685, 1671, 1910).
  - Overflow counters are at :494-495.
  - Hash: :6528.
- D193 (M DECISIONS.md:8418-8440): round 60 seeds 6002 and 6003 overflowed CandCap, with 6,257,897 and 19,008
  body-steps at crowds of 16,000-18,000. The cap is being raised to 512 or 1,024 before round 61.
- Could reef geometry share it? Not well (inference):
  1. The body grid's cell size is re-derived every step from the mean body radius. Static reef pieces would
     be re-entered every step, or the cell would be set by the pieces.
  2. Reef pieces in the candidate lists would compete for CandCap exactly where D193 found it binding: the
     crowds live on the islands, and the corals would be there too.
  3. The body grid is 3D and hashed over live bodies. Reefs are static and mostly vertical.
- Recommended instead: a SEPARATE static structure, built once at world creation alongside WR. It can reuse
  the same CSR layout and the same Hash, or be a plain 2D (x, z) column grid. Each cell lists the piece ids
  whose reach disc overlaps the cell, in ascending order. A query reads one cell's list.
  Cost per query is about the number of pieces near the point, not N. Inference: it is bit-identical to the
  brute scan as long as every piece whose bound can be under the best is listed, and lists are in ascending
  index order. A brute-versus-grid test proves it on CPU and card.

5. THE THEATRE (must never stand outside the collider)
======================================================================================================
- Meshes: TheatreReefRock builds a lathe cap and a lathe stem per reef, with noise cut INWARD only along
  the analytic surface's inward direction. The bounds are top plus underside at most 0.6 t, the rim 0.45 of
  its half-thickness, the stem 0.4 of its radius. The fillet is drawn by bisection on the same smooth-min
  formula, pulled in by 3%.
  M unity/Assets/Theatre/TheatreReefRock.cs:9-47; resolution of 96-256 segments, about 0.2 m, at :53-64.
- ReefLook.From reads OutlineRadius, CapTopY, CapUndersideY, StemRadius and NoiseSeed: M
  unity/Assets/Theatre/ReefLook.cs:88-118. It assumes a lathe: a cap plus a stem.
- The theatre re-places the reefs from the run's config and seed, as World does (Place with worldRules
  false): M unity/Assets/Theatre/SnapshotWorld.cs:289-320. A placement tied to islands would have to rebuild
  the island map there too.
- The shafts are shaded by at most 32 cap discs (centre, widest radius, underside):
  M unity/Assets/Theatre/TheatreShaftMarch.shader:134-160; M unity/Assets/Theatre/TheatreSkin.cs:803,
  1233-1247. Round 60 has 53 reefs, so reefs 33-53 cast no shaft shade today (inference from the loop at
  TheatreSkin.cs:1240). The disc is an approximation already.
- One holder and mesh set per reef, with a shared rock material and a property block per reef
  (TheatreSkin.cs:421-425, BuildReefs from :1597).
- What a new shape asks of it:
  - a mesh generator for the new primitive, with displacement inward only;
  - a shade representation for the shafts: better a 2D transmission texture than a disc array;
  - SnapshotWorld rebuilding the shape, and the island map if placement uses it;
  - level of detail for films with many pieces.
- One useful fact for branching shapes. The physics' smooth union lies OUTSIDE the plain union: SmoothUnion
  subtracts k h^3/6 from the min (ReefGeometry.cs:1061-1075). So a mesh of the plain union of the pieces is
  always inside the collider, and joints need no fillet drawn to stay legal (inference from the formula).
- The analysis scripts read reefs from run.json as mushrooms: M scripts/reads/r47-read.py through
  r54-read.py, tables.py, snow-read.py, theatre-snap.ps1 and others. A new shape needs new readers.

7. PLACEMENT ON THE ISLANDS (common to every option; the coordinator's requirement)
======================================================================================================
How to tie the placement to the islands' footprint as seeded:
- Build the island map before the reefs. On R61 it is a pure function of the landscape seed and the
  wavelength (R61 World.cs:1879-1880), and the reefs come from the same landscape seed (:1689). Moving the
  GradientNoise construction above ReefGeometry.Place is enough.
- Take the threshold the way PlanIslands does (GridField.cs:1495-1570): the (1 - cover) quantile over the
  matter grid's columns (5 m), inside the glass less the 30 m margin, and not filled by the bed. Compute it
  WITHOUT the reef mask. A small circularity exists (inference): in the 5 m matter grid, a stem column under
  a cap whose top is above 2.5 m has every cell centre in rock, so that column is not "live" to the real
  PlanIslands. A reef-free threshold can differ from the seeding's by those few columns.
- Two ways out:
  (a) state the reef's island test as its own rule on the reef-free columns, and accept that a few columns
      differ;
  (b) change PlanIslands to count the reef-free columns as well. That is a new realisation of every reef
      world with islands (rounds 60 and 61), so it is the owner's ruling.
- Draw rule: a tunable ReefIslandShare s in [0, 1] (illustrative name). Each colony draws, from the reef
  stream, whether it is an island colony (with probability s) or a desert one. Its centre is then
  rejection-sampled until map(x, z) is above the threshold (island) or below it (desert), with today's
  4,000-try refusal. Optionally weight island centres by the plan's weight, so plateau columns are favoured
  over the shore ramp (GridField.cs:1576-1582).

What share can sit on islands against the open water:
- Areas: the islands are about 8,400-9,800 m2 (round 60 read, section 1). Today's caps cover about 22,000 m2
  (0.25 of 88,000). The desert is the remaining roughly 78,000 m2.
- A cap gives zero light under it (ReefGeometry.cs:1213-1219). Leaves seed and feed on the islands in the top
  12 m. A coral canopy over an island therefore takes light from the oasis's own producers. The coral budget
  on islands is best stated as a share of each island's footprint, for example 10-30% roofed (estimate,
  owner's call). It should not be stated as a share of the tank.
- At 20% of 9,000 m2, that is about 1,800 m2 of island canopy: about 4-5 of today's mean caps (pi x 11^2
  is about 380 m2), or many more small colonies (estimate).
- If s = 1 at today's cover, the caps would need 2.2-2.6 times the islands' whole area. That cannot be met
  without heavy overlap, and the oases would go dark. Hence the cover dial needs restating for islands.
- Desert colonies at s < 1 serve as stepping stones and shade in the open water. The owner has held
  strands or stepping stones between islands for a later round (D192, M DECISIONS.md:8403-8405), so a
  desert share is itself a world-rule question.

The 30 m glass margin:
- Islands are at least 30 m off the glass (RunConfig.cs:1563-1573; GridField.cs:1508-1538). The reef rule is
  only the outline 2 m inside the glass (ReefGeometry.cs:63, 623).
- On island colonies the 30 m margin binds. A colony centred on an island column still reaches out by its
  own radius, so its rock lies at least about 30 - r_reach m from the glass: 14-24 m for reaches of 6-16 m
  (estimate). Nothing to change.
- Desert colonies today may stand 2 m from the glass. Recommend the same 30 m margin for them, so the wall
  does not become a habitat. D162 kept the grip off the glass for this reason, and D185 kept the islands off
  it.
- Depth interacts too. DeepEnough needs depth + t + 2 m, about 8 m at the defaults, under the cap's centre
  and outline (ReefGeometry.cs:735-749). Round 60's bed has shelves at 6/12/24 m over 1.2/12.4/46.9% of the
  floor, so an island over a shallow shelf may refuse mushroom caps. Bed-rooted branching colonies (options
  3-5) instead stand on the bed's height and suit shallow islands better (inference).

======================================================================================================
8. DESIGN OPTIONS, CHEAPEST TO RICHEST
======================================================================================================
All costs are ESTIMATES, unmeasured. "R0" means today's reef work per link per physics step on the card:
contact plus water at 53 lobed reefs. Its share of the card step is itself unmeasured
(card-audit-r57.md:880-882). Measuring it is mitigation zero for every option: run the probe with the reefs
on and off on one checkpoint, after the round is done.

Every option adds tunables. A new build therefore refuses every older config, genome-world and checkpoint
under the loading rule (M CLAUDE.md gotcha; RunConfig.cs:2000-2005). The recorded fixtures are re-recorded
(src/Evosim.Core/CLAUDE.md). Old mushroom runs replay only on their own builds. The theatre must keep the
mushroom path to draw them, since it re-places reefs from the config (SnapshotWorld.cs:296-320). Each option
is deterministic as a pure function of the config and the landscape seed, on its own stream. Bit-identity
between CPU and card needs the mul.rn transcription in the kernel.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTION 1. Rougher, trig-free lobed caps on the islands (cheapest)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What: keep the mushroom. Raise the harmonics from k = 2..4 to 2..8, so more lobes and an embayed rim. Replace
Atan2 + sin/cos by multiple-angle recurrences on (nx, nz) = (cos t, sin t), which are already computed
(ReefGeometry.cs:875-876): cos kt and sin kt follow from Chebyshev recurrences, so no trig at all. Add island
placement (section 7).
- Card: per ReefOf about 0.3-0.8 C without the CORDIC, against 1.1-1.3 C if Atan2 is kept with more
  harmonics. Loops are unchanged at N about 50. Net about 0.5-1.0 R0 (estimate). It may come out CHEAPER
  than today.
- CPU: the same ratio. One-time costs (mask, edge fades) are unchanged.
- Steeper lobes lower cosMin, which widens reach (ReefGeometry.cs:187, 198) and puts more links in reach.
  The first-order distance error grows with curvature (:25-33), and RefuseFlat refuses lobes thinner than
  t/2 (:265). Keep OutlineSwing at 0.4.
- Determinism and old runs: a new realisation (Atan2 removed); the new k range adds tunables.
- Theatre: smallest work. The lathe already takes any outline function (ReefLook.cs:112). Raise the segment
  count for k = 8 (TheatreReefRock.cs:56). Replace the 32-disc shaft array with a texture, which is needed
  already at 53 reefs.
- Creatures: a longer rim, nooks under the lobes, and the same tables, shade and snow traps. No new perches,
  and no change to D171's still water over the caps. Not coral-like beyond the outline.
- Islands: s of colonies on islands. The island budget is 4-5 mean caps at 20% roofed (estimate). Unless the
  caps are made smaller there, few large caps darken big pieces of each oasis.
- Mitigations: trig-free outline; card sharing of the fade between velocity and acceleration (bit-neutral by
  the CPU's own claim, CurrentField.cs:1083); a gradient-only contact path.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTION 2. Tiered tabular clusters: many small caps per colony, at stepped depths, on the islands
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What: a colony is 3-8 of today's units (cap plus stem), smaller (r about 1.5-5 m), at depths stepped from
1 to 8 m, packed around one island point. This is the look of tabular Acropora stands. It reuses
ReefGeometry wholesale, with the Option 1 maths. N rises to about 100-400 units (estimate).
- Card without a grid: the contact's two LB passes and the water's two reach scans grow linearly. At N = 300,
  about 6 times today's scans, roughly 2-4 R0 (estimate).
- Card with a static 2D grid over the units' reach discs (cell about 8 m, ascending lists): about 1-2 R0,
  the cost being the units near the point (estimate).
- CPU: the same per-step ratios. The one-time mask scan (about 2.8M cells x N LB) goes from about 0.15G to
  about 0.8G LB at N = 300: seconds, once (estimate). With the grid it is near today's.
- Light, corpses and placement: each is O(N) per call (sections l, m, n). Use the same grid, or a baked 2D
  canopy map (one float per 1 m column) that replaces the per-call loop. The canopy map is cheaper than
  today's LightTransmission even at 53 reefs.
- Matter fields: more split columns, and more intervals for InColumn (section k). Small caps under 5 m kill
  few 5 m matter cells, so snow passes many of them (inference).
- Determinism and old runs: new tunables (units per colony, depth steps, size range, island share).
  Otherwise ReefGeometry's own maths.
- Theatre: the same lathe per unit, so many more small meshes. Instancing or LOD per colony, and the shade
  texture.
- Creatures: perches and tables at several lit depths; dappled shade with transmission 0 under each table
  and light between them; snow traps on each table; gaps between stems as size refuges. The fade still stills
  the water around each unit, and around a dense cluster the whole thicket is still (D171's problem, inside
  the oasis). This may be good for refuge and bad for filter-feeding holdfasts (inference).
- Islands: the natural fit. A small footprint per unit lets 10-30% of each island be roofed in many pieces
  rather than one dark hole.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTION 3. Branching colonies of capsules (staghorn-like), a hull fade, and a baked canopy
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What: a colony is a short tree grown from the bed at an island point: a trunk, and branches by a fixed
recursive rule from the reef stream (angle, length decay, radius taper), 15-60 capsules per colony, 10-40
colonies. Optionally, flat plates at some tips for tables.
- Each piece is a capsule (sphere-swept segment). Its SDF needs one projection, a clamp and one sqrt, with
  NO trig. The gradient is a unit vector, and the Hessian is (I - n n^T)/d, or that minus the axis term on the
  side. The Hessian jumps at the cylinder-to-sphere seam, as today's cap does on q = 0
  (ReefGeometry.cs:43-46).
- Pieces within a colony join by the same cubic smooth minimum. Colonies join by min.
- Split the readers by need:
  - CONTACT (and touch, grip, guard, placement) uses the true capsule union, so bodies meet branches.
  - The STREAMS' fade uses one smooth hull per colony (an ellipsoid, or today's cap-plus-stem envelope).
    The water cost is then per colony, not per branch.
  - The LIGHT uses a baked 2D canopy map: transmission per 1 m column, from the projected branches and
    plates, baked once.
- Card:
  - Contact via a static grid of pieces: about 5-30 capsule tests per query, each about the cost of one of
    today's LBs, so about 0.5-1 R0.
  - Water via hull fades: about 0.3-0.8 R0 (no Atan2).
  - Total about 1-2 R0 with the grid and the hull, against 5-30 R0 with no grid and a fade per branch, which
    is unacceptable (estimates).
  - Memory: about 8 Reals a capsule; 1,500 pieces is about 12,000 Reals, trivial (estimate).
- CPU: the same ratios. One-time costs: the mask is cheap because thin branches kill few cells; the edge
  fades read the hull.
- Matter fields: branches of radius 0.2-1 m kill almost no 1 m or 5 m cell. Snow passes through the coral
  and does not collect on it, unlike the tables (0118:110-121). D190's burial is untouched (inference).
  Plates thicker than a cell would bring the tables back.
- Determinism and old runs: new primitives and new tunables, so a new realisation and older configs refused.
  The kernel needs new transcribed functions with mul.rn. Bits are kept only if the grid lists stay in
  ascending piece order.
- Theatre: a new tube and plate mesh generator, with noise inward only, bounded at 0.4 of the radius as the
  stem is today (TheatreReefRock.cs:25). Joints need no fillet, since the plain union is inside the smooth
  union (section 5). LOD for films. The shade texture comes from the same canopy map as the simulation, so
  the shafts and the physics agree. SnapshotWorld must rebuild the colonies and the map.
- Creatures:
  - Surface at every depth and facing. A drifting weightless body meets a vertical branch from the side,
    which it cannot do with a horizontal table. That bears directly on D171's null, where nothing touched
    the cap tops (inference).
  - Perches at the lit depth for gripping grazers.
  - Dappled shade, with no full dark under a sparse canopy.
  - Size refuges: gaps narrower than a large body keep predators out.
  - The hull fade makes the thicket's interior slow water: a refuge, and slower drift past.
  Area: about 7.5 m2 a capsule (r 0.4 m, 3 m long), so 40 capsules is about 300 m2 per colony, against
  about 1,150 m2 for a mean mushroom (estimate). Less area, but far more contact geometry per m2 of footprint.
- Dial worth adding: a FLOOR on the hull fade (g >= g_min > 0 inside the hull). Water then passes through
  the thicket slowly, and a holdfast inside it still receives drifting matter. g_min = 0 is today's behaviour.
  The streams' g u + grad g x A stays divergence-free for any smooth g (ReefGeometry.cs:49-53). Rock is still
  impenetrable to bodies by contact (inference). The Courant check (GridField.cs:2955-2972) must be re-read,
  because short or steep fades raise the contour current, which scales as |A|/fade (RunConfig.cs:2146-2156).
- Islands: grow colonies only from island columns at share s. Budget them by canopy share per island, not by
  count.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTION 4. A baked distance field in sparse bricks, sampled by the kernels (any shape)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What: grow any shape on the CPU at world creation (L-system branches, noise-carved massive corals, boulders,
overhangs and caves). Bake its signed distance on a fine grid only in bricks near the rock: islands times
coral height. The kernels sample the table.
- Smoothness is the catch. The streams need C2 with an exact Hessian (ReefGeometry.cs:39-46). Trilinear
  interpolation gives a C0 gradient and a zero Hessian, which breaks the closed-form acceleration and makes
  the contour current step. A C2 reader needs a tricubic B-spline: 64 taps, with the rock DEFINED as the
  spline's zero set so every reader agrees.
  - Cheaper hybrid: the baked field only for the CONTACT (trilinear, 8 taps, gradient only), with a hull fade
    for the water as in Option 3.
- Card:
  - Tricubic: about 64 loads plus about 300 flops for S, G and H, roughly 1-3 R0 per sample (estimate).
  - The hybrid: about 0.5-1 R0.
  - Memory: about 9,000 m2 of islands x 12 m tall at 0.25 m is about 6.9M voxels, about 28 MB as float; at
    0.5 m about 3.5 MB (estimate). Small against the 13,000-18,000 MiB a round-60 group needs
    (M HANDOFF.md:199-200). It does change the card's upload and checkpoint path.
- CPU: the same per-sample cost. The bake is once, seconds to minutes (estimate). The bake must be
  deterministic in doubles and stored as float, and both engines read the same table.
- Determinism and old runs: new tunables, and a new data surface that is not in config.json. Record its hash
  in the manifest as the reefs are recorded today (Manifest.cs:152-161). An identity claim then also covers
  the table.
- Theatre: marching cubes on the same table at an inward iso offset larger than the interpolation error, so
  the mesh never stands outside the collider. A displacement shader with inward-only noise.
- Creatures: the richest geometry, with caves, overhangs and porous massive corals. Grip, perches and
  refuges as in Option 3, plus enclosed dark spaces. Its light needs a baked canopy map too.
- Islands: bricks exist only over the islands' footprint, so the memory is bounded by the islands' area, and
  desert corals cost bricks of their own.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTION 5. Option 3 or 4 with holdfasts on: edge corals that feed the anchoring cell (richest)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What: the branching colonies of Option 3 (or 4), placed by preference on the islands' EDGES (map values just
above the threshold, the shore ramp of GridField.cs:1576-1582) where matter drifts off. The hull fade has a
floor, so water crosses the coral. AnchoringCell is re-enabled by its existing settings (D165 weight).
- This is HANDOFF's open question (M HANDOFF.md:195-198). It reopens D171, so it is the owner's ruling and
  comes after round 61.
- Card and CPU: Option 3's costs, plus the grip's per-anchored-link spring (Anchor.cs). It is cheap, and
  independent of the shape because it reads only RockTouched. If the fade floor is set low or per piece,
  contour-current substeps may rise (estimate, read on the Courant ceiling).
- Determinism and old runs: as Option 3, plus the anchoring tunables, which already exist.
- Theatre: as Option 3. Bodies gripping branches need the joint and contact pictures checked against a
  reference (the root CLAUDE.md's render check).
- Creatures: a reason to hold on (food delivered by moving water to a fixed place on the oasis rim);
  sit-and-wait niches; shade; refuges; a ring of rock that marks each oasis for migrants.
- Islands: edge-weighted placement at share s, with interiors kept open for the leaves' light.

10. MITIGATIONS THAT BOUND THE COST (which options need which)
======================================================================================================
1. Measure first: probe a round 60 or 61 checkpoint with the reefs on and off. The reef share of the water
   thread is unknown (card-audit-r57.md:880-882). All options.
2. Drop Atan2 (Chebyshev recurrences on nx and nz) or use primitives without trig. Options 1 and 2; 3-5
   avoid it by construction.
3. Share the fade between the card's VelocityAt and AccelerationAt, as the CPU does (gpu-kernel.template:
   2139/2156 against CurrentField.cs:1083-1101). This halves the water's reef work. Bit-neutral by the CPU's
   own claim (inference). Today, for every option.
4. A gradient-only contact path, skipping the Hessian (inference: bit-neutral). All options.
5. A static grid over the reef pieces (2D columns, about 8 m cells, ascending lists, built once and uploaded
   with WR). Kept separate from the body broad phase, so the pieces never compete for CandCap (D193).
   Options 2-5.
6. Bounding volumes per colony: a reach disc and a vertical slab test before any piece is read, as
   LowerBound does for a reef now (ReefGeometry.cs:844-858). Options 2-5.
7. A hull fade per colony instead of per piece, so the water cost scales with colonies, not branches.
   Options 3-5.
8. A baked 2D canopy map for the light, the corpses' resting height and the theatre's shafts. One float per
   column replaces the O(N) loops at World.cs:560, :3583-3596 and LightTransmission. Options 2-5.
9. A cap on pieces per colony and on canopy share per island (refused when it is passed, like ReefMaxCount),
   so the cost is bounded by configuration, not by chance. Options 2-5.
10. Level of detail is for the theatre only. The simulation must read one shape (no LOD in physics), or
    identity is lost.
11. A baked distance field only where it pays: contact-only, in bricks over the islands. Option 4.
