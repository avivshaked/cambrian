# Sources — early-life mechanics

Retrieval record. Every claim in [`MECHANISMS.md`](MECHANISMS.md) resolves to a key here.

**"Read" says how much of the source the claim actually rests on.** A claim drawn from an abstract
is not a claim drawn from a paper, and the difference is invisible once it has been paraphrased —
so it is recorded here rather than remembered. All retrieved **2026-08-29** unless noted.

---

### [BORMANS99] — buoyancy regulation and the light/nutrient separation
- Bormans, M., Sherman, B. S. & Webster, I. T. (1999). *Is buoyancy regulation in cyanobacteria an
  adaptation to exploit separation of light and nutrients?* **Marine & Freshwater Research** 50(8),
  897–906.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1071/MF99105
- URL used: https://connectsci.au/mf/article-lookup/doi/10.1071/MF99105
  (redirected from `publish.csiro.au/mf/MF99105`, HTTP 301)
- **Read: abstract only.** Paywalled body not retrieved.
- Load-bearing for §1. It **questions** the Fogg & Walsby (1971) hypothesis: evidence for population
  migration to exploit the separation is "tenuous", most field observations show no migration deep
  enough to reach nutrients, and "algal buoyancy appears to be dependent much more on light than on
  nutrients", consistent with the carbohydrate-ballast mechanism.

### [MICROCYSTIS11] — the three buoyancy mechanisms, and which dominates
- *Heterogeneity of buoyancy in response to light between two buoyant types of cyanobacterium
  Microcystis*. **Hydrobiologia** (2011).
- URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10750-011-0894-y
- **Read: search-result summary only.** Not fetched directly.
- Supports §1's mechanism description: buoyancy is regulated by gas-vesicle collapse under turgor,
  by regulation of gas-vesicle formation, or by carbohydrate ballast; carbohydrate fluctuates on
  shorter timescales than gas vesicles, so short-term migration is mostly ballast and gas vesicles
  are the longer-term adjustment.

### [VIRALSHUNT] — recycling without a consumer
- *Viral shunt*. **Wikipedia**, citing Wilhelm & Suttle (1999), *BioScience*.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viral_shunt
- **Read: full article.** ⚠ Encyclopaedia, not a primary source — the Wilhelm & Suttle paper was
  not retrieved, so the 25% figure is reported at one remove.
- Load-bearing for §2: as much as **25% of global phytoplankton primary production** may be recycled
  in the microbial loop via the shunt; 20–40% of bacterial biomass becomes virus-infected; the shunt
  converts particulate organic matter to **dissolved** organic matter, which microbes take up.

### [REDFIELD] — one nutrient is not enough
- *Redfield ratio*. **Wikipedia**. C:N:P ≈ 106:16:1.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redfield_ratio
- **Read: search-result summary only.**
- Companion, on serial co-limitation (relieving N shifts limitation to P):
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12993680/ — **read: search-result summary only.**

### [GOE] — waste that rebuilt the planet
- *The Great Oxidation Event: How Cyanobacteria Changed Life*. **ASM**.
  https://asm.org/articles/2022/february/the-great-oxidation-event-how-cyanobacteria-change
- *The Great Oxygenation Event as a consequence of ecological dynamics modulated by planetary
  change*. **Nature Communications** (2021).
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-23286-7
- **Read: search-result summaries only.** Neither fetched directly.
- Supports §4: O₂ was metabolic **waste**; mineral sinks absorbed it until they saturated; it then
  poisoned the anaerobic incumbents, and the same molecule became the fuel for aerobic metabolism.

### [MATS] — the sea floor as habitat
- *Signatures of early microbial life from the Archean (4 to 2.5 Ga) eon*. **Earth-Science Reviews**.
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012825220303421
- *Controls on development and diversity of Early Archean stromatolites*. **PNAS** (2009).
  https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0903323106
- **Read: search-result summaries only.**
- Supports §5: benthic microbial mats from **3.47 Ga**; binding, biostabilisation, baffling and
  trapping of sediment build microbially induced sedimentary structures and stromatolites.

### [PREDATION] — why carnivory is late
- *On the evolutionary emergence of predation*. **Journal of Theoretical Biology** (2023).
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022519323001753
- Sperling et al., *Oxygen, ecology, and the Cambrian radiation of animals*. **PNAS** (2013).
  https://historical-geobiology.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj25131/files/media/file/sperling_2013_pnas_cambrian_oxygen_and_ecology_final.pdf
- **Read: search-result summaries only.**
- Supports §6: predation requires prior diversification and **biomass accumulation of prey** plus an
  oxygen threshold; modern-like ocean O₂ first at ~521 Ma.

### [PHOTOFERRO] — a producer for the dark
- *Photoferrotrophy: Remains of an Ancient Photosynthesis in Modern Environments*. **PMC**.
  https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5359306/
- **Read: search-result summary only.**
- Supports the appendix: under ferruginous, sulfide-poor Archean conditions photoferrotrophy may
  have accounted for most photic-zone primary production, and was progressively **pushed to greater
  depths** as cyanobacteria oxygenated the surface.

## Honest limits of this set

- **Nothing here was read as a full paper except the two encyclopaedia articles.** Six of eight keys
  rest on search-result summaries or abstracts. That is enough to decide *what to look into*; it is
  not enough to put a number in `DESIGN.md`. Any parameter taken from this area must be traced to a
  full text first.
- **No systematic search protocol was run.** These are targeted queries, not a review. There is no
  claim of coverage, and a mechanism absent from this list is not thereby absent from the biology.
- **Two encyclopaedia sources are load-bearing** ([VIRALSHUNT], [REDFIELD]) for quantitative claims.
  Replace with primaries before either informs a tunable.
