# Commercial use

**2026-09-07**  ·  what you may do with this project without asking, what needs a licence, and how to ask

This repository is published under two non-commercial licences. The PolyForm Noncommercial
License 1.0.0 covers the code and the evolved genomes ([`LICENSE`](LICENSE)); CC BY-NC 4.0
covers the written work ([`LICENSE-DOCS`](LICENSE-DOCS)). Both say the same thing in their own
words. You may take the project, run it, change it, share it and build on it for any
purpose that is not commercial. Commercial use needs a licence from the copyright holder,
Aviv Shaked. This page is the plain-language companion to the two licence files. Where it
and a licence differ, the licence wins.

## What you may do without asking

- **Run it, fork it, change it and share your changes**, with attribution, for personal
  study, a hobby, curiosity or the public good.
- **Contribute to it.** [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) says how, and what you grant
  when you do.
- **Teach with it and research with it** at a school, a university, a public research
  organisation, a charity or a government body, whatever funds that institution. The code
  licence says this in so many words, and the same reading applies to the prose.
- **Cite it, quote it and review it** in a paper, a talk, a thesis, a blog or a book. Fair
  dealing and fair use are not limited by either licence, and attribution is welcome
  either way. If you build on the literature review, cite the papers it rests on as well
  as this repository.

## What needs a licence

A use is commercial when it is primarily intended for commercial advantage or monetary
compensation. Some cases that fall on that side:

- A product or a service built on the code or the genomes, whether it is sold, subscribed
  to or supported by advertising.
- Use inside a company's product development or research, including a corporate research
  lab. A university lab is non-commercial; a company's is not.
- Paid teaching material or a paid course that reproduces the primer, the design or the
  review at length rather than quoting it.
- Selling or licensing runs, genomes or trained artefacts produced with the code.
- Consulting work delivered with the code or the written work as the deliverable.

If you are not sure which side a use falls on, ask. The answer costs nothing and is usually
quick.

## How to ask

Open an issue on the repository titled "Commercial licence" saying what you want to do, or
contact the author through the repository's GitHub profile. Commercial licences are
granted case by case; there is no fixed fee and no form.

## Two things worth knowing

The copyright holder is not bound by these licences. A licence is a grant from the holder
to everyone else, so the holder may use the work commercially and may license it to
others on any terms.

Versions of this repository published before 2026-09-07 carried the MIT License and
CC BY 4.0. Both permit commercial use, and neither can be withdrawn for the copies already
made. The terms above apply from the relicensing commit onward.
