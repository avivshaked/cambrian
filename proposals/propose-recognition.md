# Proposal: every animal smells of what it is and whose it is, so food, foes and kin are one sense

*Draft for the owner, 2026-10-30. They asked for a proposal grounded in how recognition works and how it evolved, tying
together finding food, avoiding predators and deciding whether to eat one's own kin. It builds on the read of the
`r63g` screen and fits beside the matter-kinds proposal. World rules are the owner's; this is what they rule on.
Absorbed into DECISIONS.md on ruling, then deleted.*

## The proposal in one paragraph

Today no sense in the world carries identity. A mouth that touches tissue learns its cell type, its joules and whether it
is alive, and nothing else. A brain smells living leaves, remains and marine snow, but no living animal, so prey reacts
only once it is bitten, and a predator finds prey by bumping into it. My proposal is one mechanism. Every animal gives
off an odour in proportion to its tissue, and the odour carries a short heritable signature of three numbers that
drifts a little at every birth. A body then smells two things: how much animal is near, and how unlike itself that
animal is. A mouth reads the same signature in what it touches, and a heritable restraint decides whether it bites. A
body that is bitten, or that is digesting a kill, leaks an alarm odour carrying the victim's signature. Food, foes and
kin become three readings of one smell. Whether a body spares its kin is then left to evolution, and the first screen
measures what evolution does. That screen also tests the owner's claim that an asexual world gives no strong reason not
to eat one's own kind.

## 1. What the world shows now

In `r63g`'s arm B, mouths cost a tenth of their usual price. Grazers took 0.45 of their bite income from leaves and
0.49 from other living bodies. The control took 0.68 and 0.22. 57 % of grazer deaths were kills by other
grazers. Of those kills, 16 % were close kin. A parent ate its child in 7.8 %, a sibling ate a sibling in 6.2 %, and a
child ate its parent in 2.1 % (`logbook/specs/r63g-read/cannibal-r63g-b-6202.txt`). Cousins were counted as unrelated,
so the true share of kin is higher. A quarter of the victims were under 60 s old, and a third were 300 s or older.

The same read found that grazers swim but do not steer to food. The share of each 10 s move toward the nearest leaf was
−0.01, as for drifting leaves. A newborn starts a median 1.5 m from a leaf, so in this carpet of leaves steering does
not pay. No sense proposed here will make grazers steer to leaves while leaves
are everywhere. What it gives food-finding is a smell a meat-eater can follow and a smell a grazer can flee.

## 2. What the evidence says about recognition

### Recognition compares a cue with a template

Students of kin recognition name four routes [LLH20]. Spatial rules treat whatever is in the nest as kin. Familiarity
treats as kin what an animal grew up with. Phenotype matching compares a stranger's cue with a template taken from
the animal itself or its kin, so it can recognise kin it has never met. Recognition alleles, the "greenbeards", tie the
cue, the template and the response to one gene [GW10]. Phenotype matching needs a cue that tracks relatedness, and
familiarity needs a period of learning.

Fish use smell for both. Juvenile Arctic charr prefer the water of siblings that share their genotype at the major
histocompatibility complex (MHC), a set of immune genes. The authors conclude that the MHC has a significant effect on
the odours charr use to recognise kin [OGL98]. Zebrafish larvae imprint on kin odour in a 24-hour window at day 6 after
fertilisation. Larvae raised alone do not imprint on their own odour, and larvae kept with unrelated fish do not
imprint on them [GHA08]. The template is learned, then, but the brain is primed to learn it from kin.

### Cannibals that can recognise kin mostly spare them

Spadefoot toad tadpoles can develop into a cannibal form. Later work reports that cannibals preferentially ate
non-siblings, while the omnivore form preferentially joined siblings [PRS93, as reported in PSC94 and PF99]. Arizona
tiger salamanders were studied for the same choice [PSC94]. A later study weighed discrimination against the risk of
harming kin. Species and families most likely to produce cannibals were most likely to avoid eating kin. A tadpole
discriminated while in its cannibal form and stopped after it reverted [PF99]. I read this as Hamilton's rule at work in
a mouth: the more a body can harm kin, the more it pays to know them.

Parents also eat their young. A review of this filial cannibalism in bony fish separates eating the whole brood from
eating part of it. It weighs an energy-based explanation that some studies support and others dispute [MA02].
Cannibalism is widespread: Polis reviewed over 900 papers covering about 1,300 species [PO81]. A model shows it can even
rescue a starving population. This "lifeboat" works when one eaten juvenile yields more than one new offspring at low
density, in species whose juveniles eat food the adults do not [BRG88]. Disease is often named as a cost of eating one's
own kind. A review found cannibalism the main route of transmission in only two known cases [RA07].

### Clonal organisms recognise self by matching alleles

In the social amoeba *Dictyostelium*, a matching pair of alleles at two genes, *tgrB1* and *tgrC1*, is necessary and
sufficient for cells to treat each other as self [HB11]. Wild strains mixed together partly exclude one another, and the
exclusion grows with the genetic distance between them [OK08]. That graded response is what a continuous signature
gives. The bacterium *Proteus mirabilis* draws a visible line between swarming colonies of different strains, using a
six-gene locus whose products mark identity [GUG08]. The colonial sea squirt *Botryllus* fuses with a neighbour colony
that shares an allele at one highly variable locus, and rejects one that shares none [DT05]. The gene behind it is
disputed. Fusing has a price: in a fused colony, unrelated germ cells compete for the gonads [SRW99].

### Kin recognition erodes its own cues

Crozier argued that recognition by genetic cues should destroy the variation it depends on [CR86, as framed by HZL13].
A body with a common cue is recognised as kin, and helped or spared, more often. Common cues win, cues become alike, and
recognition stops working. Two resolutions are on offer. One is a second job for the cue that favours rare variants,
such as the MHC's role against parasites. The other is mating with unlike partners [HZL13]. This world is asexual, so
the second is closed to it. Its cue stays diverse only through mutation and through anything that makes a rare
signature pay. A predator that evolves to target the commonest prey signature would be such a thing. That is my
inference, and no paper here tests it.

### Predators are recognised by what they leak and what they ate

A kairomone is a chemical that benefits the animal that smells it, at the cost of the one that releases it. Water fleas
(*Daphnia*) migrate downward by day when they smell fish, and the cue is a fish bile salt, active at picomolar strengths
[HWE19]. Fish also react to an alarm substance from the torn skin of their own kind, von Frisch's *Schreckstoff*. Part
of it is fragments of chondroitin, a sugar polymer abundant in skin [MK12]. The skin cells that hold it are kept for
other reasons. Their numbers rise with skin pathogens, parasites and ultraviolet light, and predation pressure does not
change them. The authors conclude that the alarm role is secondary [CW07]. An alarm cue is therefore a leak and needs
no altruism to evolve.

Prey also learn predators from these cues. Naive fathead minnows were frightened by water from pike that had eaten
minnows, and not by water from pike that had eaten swordtails [MS93]. Minnows that saw an unfamiliar fish while smelling
alarm substance later fled from it [CS94]. A review covers kairomones, alarm cues and diet cues together [FWC10]. The
brains in this world do not learn during a lifetime (D146), so here any such association has to be inherited.

### Chemotaxis is a counting problem

A cell judges a concentration by counting molecules, and the noise of the count limits how well it can judge in a given
time [BP77]. The nose (D235) already treats smell this way, with noise falling as the square root of its area. This
proposal leaves that alone.

## 3. The owner's claim, tested

The owner holds that with one parent and mutated copies for offspring, nothing strongly selects against eating one's
own kind. They add that it may make the kind stronger. Some of this holds and some does not.

Hamilton's rule says an act spreads when its benefit to the actor outweighs its cost to others, each weighted by
relatedness [HA64]. For cannibalism the eater gains a meal and the victim loses its future. In `r63g`'s worlds a killer
keeps 0.8 of what it swallows, since 0.2 of every intake is wasted. Write *E* for the joules the victim holds and *w* for
the offspring the eater makes per joule it keeps. Write *V* for the offspring the victim would still have made, and *r*
for the relatedness of the two.

```
eating pays when   0.8 · E · w  >  r · V
```

For a parent and child, or two siblings, *r* is close to 1. The rule then asks whether the eater turns 0.8 of the
victim's joules into more offspring than the victim would have made itself.

Three parts of the claim hold. Where the victim would die anyway, *V* is near zero and eating it pays. In arm A, 71 % of
grazers starved at a median age of 72 s. Most newborns there were doomed, and a parent eating one recovers 0.8 of
its matter. Next, a cost that nature is thought to add, disease, is missing here, and it is weaker than often assumed
[RA07]. Last, when relatives compete for the same food, the competition can cancel the benefit of sparing them
[WPG02]. Under D261's fixed total of matter, kin may come to compete harder, and eating kin will then cost less.

Two parts do not hold. A healthy adult kin is worth more alive. Its *V* is at least its own joules turned into offspring,
plus all it would still earn, and the eater recovers only 0.8 of the joules. So with *r* near 1, eating a viable kin
loses. A third of the grazer victims were 300 s or older, and I think most were viable (inference: the read does not
show their reserves). The claim that cannibalism makes the kind stronger also fails as stated. Eating moves energy
within a clone at a loss. It strengthens the clone only if the eaten are worse than the eaters, and a mouth that bites
whatever it touches does not pick the worse. The lifeboat needs juveniles to eat food the adults do not [BRG88], and
here they eat the same leaves.

The evidence also corrects the framing. In Hamilton's rule *r* is measured against the population. If every neighbour
is a cousin, every neighbour is kin, and telling them apart buys nothing. If the water holds several unrelated clades,
a stranger is worth eating whenever the meal outweighs the risk, and a kin only when it is doomed. So the rule a body
could evolve here is to spare kin unless they are dying and to eat strangers. Tadpoles show a form of it [PF99]. The
world should not impose that rule. It should let bodies find out who is kin and let evolution decide what to do about
it.

## 4. The rules

1. **Every animal smells** of its own living tissue. Each living body emits animal odour in proportion to the volume of
   its parts that are not photosynthetic. The odour spreads as a cone, as the leaf smell does, on a grid of 2 m cells.
   The grid is filled once a metabolic step (0.5 s) and joined at the open ocean's seams (D204). A reader subtracts its
   own body's cone, so a body does not smell itself. This is the animal smell D235 designed, built now.

2. **Every genome carries a signature** of three numbers, each from 0 to 1. Founders draw theirs uniformly, and at every
   birth each number takes a Gaussian step of fixed size σ, reflected at the bounds. The step is a world tunable and not
   a gene, so selection cannot slow the drift. The signature does nothing else. It marks descent, like a stretch of
   neutral DNA.

3. **The odour carries** the signature. Each grid cell holds five sums over the bodies whose cones reach it. They are
   the odour strength, the strength times each of the three signature numbers, and the strength times the signature's
   squared length. From these a reader computes the strength-weighted mean squared distance between its signature and
   those around it, with no pass over the bodies. So a body smells how much animal is near and how unlike itself it is.

4. **A mouth tastes** what it touches. When a mouth part touches living tissue, it reads the likeness of the touched
   body's signature to its own, `L = exp(−d² / λ²)`. Here *d* is the distance between the signatures and λ a world
   tunable. The mouth pass already knows both bodies, so the taste costs one distance per contact.

5. **A heritable restraint** decides the bite. Each genome carries a restraint θ from 0 to 1, and a mouth does not bite
   living tissue whose likeness is above θ. At θ = 1 it bites everything, as today, and near 0 it spares all but
   strangers. Founders draw θ uniformly, mutated as other traits are. It applies to living tissue only, so remains are
   eaten as now: by section 3, eating the dead costs no future.

6. **A wound leaks** alarm: a body that loses tissue to a bite emits alarm odour in proportion to the joules it lost
   that step. The alarm carries its own signature into a second five-sum grid built as in rule 3. A body digesting an
   animal kill in its gut (D232) leaks the same alarm, in proportion to the animal joules left in the gut, carrying its
   prey's signature. That is the diet cue the minnows read [MS93]. Leaves leak no alarm, so D167's rejection of a pulse
   when a leaf is bitten stands.

7. **The brain reads** five new scalars. Each smelling part reads animal odour strength, animal unlikeness, alarm
   strength and alarm unlikeness, each squashed as the leaf smell is. In a world with noses, only noses read them. A
   mouth part also reads its taste. All are scalars, as D020 asks. Each is sampled once a metabolic step on the host and
   handed to both engines, so the card computes nothing new.

8. **Sight comes later** and would reuse the signature. A later sense could read a body's signature as its colour.
   DESIGN.md §5A.5 reserves part colour as an evolvable trait. Tying the two would give the signature a second job,
   the kind Crozier's argument says keeps cues diverse. It is a direction for later and not a rule now.

9. **New instruments** count, per window, the bites refused by restraint, split by close kin and others, and the kills
   by kinship, with cousins added to the cannibal tally's classes. They record the mean and spread of θ among living
   biters, the mean pairwise signature distance among living animals, and the means of the new readings. Each death row
   gains the body's signature and θ.

## 5. What a brain could do with it

The table maps the owner's three questions onto the readings. Each row is something evolution may or may not take up.
None of it is a behaviour we build.

| Question | Reading | A body that has evolved to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Where is food? | leaf smell; animal odour high, unlikeness high | a grazer follows leaves; a meat-eater follows strangers |
| Is a predator near? | alarm high, alarm unlikeness low | something is eating my kind here: swim away |
| Is a predator near? | animal odour high, unlikeness high, damage | a stranger is close and I am bitten: swim away |
| Is this kin? | taste high | the restraint spares it, or with option C the brain decides |
| Am I among kin? | animal odour high, unlikeness low | stay with the clade, or spread out from it |

## 6. What each part costs

These costs come from reading the code and the card's notes. None is measured, and the screen measures them. The `r63g` world is 300 by 300 m and 25 m deep, so a 2 m grid holds 150 × 13 × 150 = 292,500 cells. Arm B ended
with about 430 animals among 5,771 bodies. The table gives each part's cost in the farm's host step, on the card and in
the stored formats.

| Part | Farm, per metabolic step | Card | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| Animal odour, five sums | each animal adds its cone to 7³ = 343 cells at 6 m range, five floats each: about 0.7 M adds for 430 animals, 3.4 M for 2,000, against the leaf smell's 1.8 M for 5,338 leaves | none; the grid stays on the host | 5.9 MB grid, derived and not checkpointed |
| Signature and θ | none per step; read from the genome at birth | none | genome format bump |
| Taste and restraint | one distance and one comparison per mouth contact | none | none |
| Alarm, five sums | bitten bodies and full guts only, tens a step | none | one float per body, the gut's animal joules, checkpointed |
| Readings per part | 8 corners × 10 floats per smelling part: about 4 M loads for 48,000 parts (6,000 bodies of eight parts, a guess), a tenth of that with noses | one upload of five floats per part per block, about 1 MB | none |

The fills split rows over the threads and add every source in list order, as the remains smell's fill does. So the sums
are the same at any thread count. The card's notes give about 21 GB/s for a pinned array, so the per-part upload takes
about 0.05 ms a block (my arithmetic from that rate). Uploading the two grids would take about 0.6 ms a block. It would
also put new grid reads into the card's kernels, where every new product must be guarded against the fused
multiply-add. Sampling on the host avoids both. At `r63g`'s pace a simulated second takes about 385 ms of wall time, and
I expect the build to add a few per cent (inference).

## 7. What could go wrong

- **The cue may erode** if restraint pays. Common signatures would be spared more and rare ones eaten, and signatures
  may converge until likeness says nothing. That is Crozier's paradox. The fixed mutation step resupplies variation at
  every birth, the screen reads the mean pairwise distance, and σ is the dial.
- **Strangers may mimic** kin: a lineage whose signature drifts toward a cannibal clade's is spared by it, a cheat of
  the greenbeard kind, and evolution may find it. The distances between guilds over time would show it.
- **Restraint may stop** all cannibalism at once, so grazers boom and eat the leaves out. That is the overshoot D262
  asks for, and λ or the founders' θ is then dialled back.
- **Restraint may never** evolve: if θ rises toward 1, the owner's claim holds in this world, and the screen says so.
- **Everything may flee** everything: animal odour alone may push grazers apart. That suits an ocean of oases but may
  lower grazing, and the leaf-death share shows it.
- **Alarm may draw** predators: a meat-eater may evolve to follow it to a wounded body (inference; nothing I read
  tests it).
- **Self-smell may leak** if the subtraction in rule 1 is wrong. A test places one body alone and asserts it reads zero.

## 8. How this fits with matter kinds

The matter-kinds proposal decides what a meal is worth to whom. A plant-eater whose diet gene is near 0 keeps almost
nothing of animal matter, so most grazer-on-grazer bites would lose and stop. This proposal decides what a body knows
before it bites. Matter kinds removes the cheap cannibal at its cause. Recognition gives the predator lineage left
behind a target to find, and gives its prey a predator to flee. Kin restraint then matters most inside a meat-eating
lineage, where section 3's case returns. Rule 6's diet cue needs the matter kind at the swallow, the one float per body
counted above. Two levers in one screen cannot be read apart, so I recommend screening recognition first on `r63g`'s
world, with a combined arm after both read.

## 9. Options

Option A, which I recommend, is rules 1 to 7 and 9, each behind its own switch and off by default, so every recorded
world replays. It starts overpowered, per D262. No new reading carries noise, restraint is free and the taste
is exact. The step σ is 0.02 a birth, λ is 0.1, the odour reaches 6 m and the alarm 10 m. At these values a parent and child read a likeness of about 0.89 and siblings about 0.79. Bodies ten births apart read
about 0.3 and unrelated founders about 0. Uniform founders' θ means that at the start most bodies spare their children,
so evolution has to raise θ to eat kin. Record: one genome bump and one config bump, so no inoculum crosses. Rounds:
one screen of four worlds. Machine: a few per cent on the host. Risk: the cue erodes, or restraint overshoots.

Option B is A without the signature, so odour and alarm only. It gives food and foe but no kin, and is cheaper by four
sums a grid and two genes. It leaves the owner's kin question open.

Option C comes after A reads. The brain gains an output on each mouth part that can close the jaw. It works beside or
instead of the restraint gene, so hunger can change the choice as section 3 predicts. D146's brain has outputs only for
joints, so this is a new kind of output. It needs a copy of the gate on the card, and it leaves the first bite of a
touch unguarded. I would build it only if A shows restraint pays.

I rejected a signature derived from the genome by a fixed projection of its parameters. It would be hard to fake. But
body genes under strong selection would drag it, its drift could not be dialled, and a genome whose length changes has
no stable projection. A free gene with a fixed step is cleaner and has a dial.

## 10. The first screen

Four worlds on seed 6202, founded as `r63g-b`, in one card group to 24,000 s:

1. **Control** is `r63g-b` itself. A short check that the new build with every switch off repeats it bit for bit comes
   first.
2. **Kin** carries rules 1 to 5 and 7 (odour, signature, taste and restraint) without alarm.
3. **Kin without heredity** is arm 2 with a fresh random signature drawn at every birth, so likeness says nothing
   about kinship. This null separates kin recognition from restraint in general.
4. **Kin and alarm** is arm 2 plus rule 6.

The machinery works if close kin's share of grazer-on-grazer kills falls from 0.16 to under 0.08 in arm 2 and not in
arm 3. Evolution answers the owner's question through θ. If restraint pays, θ's mean among living biters falls below
0.4 by 12,000 s in arm 2. It starts at the founders' 0.5 and stays near it in arm 3. If eating kin pays, θ rises toward 1 in
arm 2, and the owner's claim stands here. Arm 4 works if bodies that read alarm move away from its source more than in
arm 2, by a motion read like `r63g`'s, and fewer grazers die to grazers. The cue holds if the mean pairwise signature
distance in arm 2 stays above half its value at 3,000 s. The build holds if the pace stays within 10 % of `r63g-b`'s
2.6 times real time and the audit residuals stay at their scale.

## The coordinator's review, 2026-10-30

- **D235's condition is not plainly met.** The control takes 0.68 of its bite income from leaves, but its grazers do not make a living: 71 % of A's grazers starve at a median 72 s, and every screen has failed D222. The owner rules this reading. The case for building the smell now rests better on D262 and the owner's question than on D235's gate.
- **The 0.8 a killer keeps** is from the intake waste of 0.2 and is not checked against the swallow in `WorldContact`. Under matter kinds it becomes the eater's efficiency for animal matter, and section 3's sum changes with it.
- **The likeness figures check.** With σ = 0.02 on each of three numbers and λ = 0.1, a parent and child read e^−0.12 ≈ 0.89, siblings e^−0.24 ≈ 0.79, ten births apart e^−1.2 ≈ 0.30, and unrelated founders about e^−50.
- **A body with leaf and other tissue smells partly of animal** under rule 1, which matches matter kinds' kind by tissue.
- **Order.** The draft recommends screening recognition alone before a combined screen with matter kinds. The owner agreed on 2026-10-30 that one unified proposal comes from both, so that order is the unified proposal's to settle.
